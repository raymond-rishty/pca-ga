<article class="home-result home-result--rpr rpr-card" data-result-item data-result-url="exc/piedmont-triad__025.html" data-result-title="Piedmont Triad — 52nd (2025) RPR exception" data-result-type="RPR exception"><span class="home-result__metadata"><span class="home-result__category">RPR exception</span><span class="home-result__separator" aria-hidden="true">•</span><span>52nd (2025)</span></span><a class="home-result__title" data-result-primary data-result-type="RPR exception" data-result-title="BCO 21-4, RAO 16-3.e.6" href="exc/piedmont-triad__025.html">BCO 21-4, RAO 16-3.e.6</a><p class="rpr-card__body">Presbytery incorrectly judged a candidate’s stated differences as merely semantic. First minute read: “I have a perceived difference with the WCF 4.1 (cf. also WLC 15; WSC 9) which states: “It pleased God the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, for the manifestation of the glory of His eternal power, wisdom, and goodness, in the beginning, to create or make of nothing the world, and all things therein, whether visible or invisible, in the space of six days, and all very good.” The Confession here appears to teach that creation took place in the span of six, literal 24 hour periods. I do not believe an orthodox view of creation necessitates the “Calendar Day Interpretation” of Genesis 1-2. That being said, I believe God called all things, both invisible and visible, out of nothing (ex nihilo) into being by his voice, as the Belgic Confession 12 states without reference to the span of time: “We believe that the Father, when it seemed good to him, created heaven and earth and all other creatures from nothing, by the Word—that is to say, by the Son.” Second minute reads: “I have a perceived difference with the WCF 21.8 (cf. also WLC 1117, 119; WSC 60), which states: “This Sabbath is to be kept holy unto the Lord when men, after a due preparing of their hearts, and ordering of their common affairs beforehand, do not only observe an holy rest all the day from their own works, words, and thoughts about their worldly employments and recreations; but also are taken up the whole time in the public and private exercises of His worship, and in the duties of necessity and mercy.” The Confession here appears to teach that personal, private activities for pleasure are not appropriate on the Sabbath. I believe that New Covenant Christians keep the Sabbath holy primarily by worshiping God weekly on the First Day (i.e. Sunday). We rest from our work and ordinary labors in order to facilitate our worship of God. Recreational activities that do not hinder our ability to worship are still permissible. This view is expressed in the Heidelberg Catechism 103: “Q. What is God’s will for you in the fourth commandment? A. First, that the gospel ministry and education for it be maintained, and that, especially on the festive day of rest, I diligently attend the assembly of God’s people to learn what God’s Word teaches, to participate in the sacraments, to pray to God publicly, and to bring Christian offerings for the poor.”” Both statements depart from the Standards’ teaching on the relevant topics, and thus are more than semantic.</p><span class="home-result__facts"><span class="home-result__fact"><b>Provision(s):</b> BCO 21-4, RAO 16-3.e.6</span><span class="home-result__fact"><b>Lifecycle:</b> raised (52nd)</span><span class="home-result__fact"><b>Final disposition:</b> raised (open)</span></span><details class="result-actions"><summary>Actions</summary><div class="result-actions__panel" aria-label="Record actions"><button type="button" data-result-action="save">Save</button><button type="button" data-result-action="cite">Cite</button><button type="button" data-result-action="link">Copy link</button></div></details></article>
<article class="home-result home-result--rpr rpr-card" data-result-item data-result-url="exc/piedmont-triad__026.html" data-result-title="Piedmont Triad — 52nd (2025) RPR exception" data-result-type="RPR exception"><span class="home-result__metadata"><span class="home-result__category">RPR exception</span><span class="home-result__separator" aria-hidden="true">•</span><span>52nd (2025)</span></span><a class="home-result__title" data-result-primary data-result-type="RPR exception" data-result-title="BCO 21-4, RAO 16-3.e.5" href="exc/piedmont-triad__026.html">BCO 21-4, RAO 16-3.e.5</a><p class="rpr-card__body">Presbytery incorrectly judged a candidate’s stated differences as merely semantic. Both stated differences actually differ from the Confession of Faith. The first difference reads: “I have a perceived difference with the WCF Chapter 21, Section 8: “The Sabbath is then kept holy unto the Lord, when men, after a due preparing of their hearts, and ordering of their affairs beforehand, do not only observe an holy rest, all the day, from their own, works, words, and thoughts about their worldly employments, and recreations, but also are taken up the whole time, in the public and private exercises of his worship, and in the duties of necessity, and mercy.” though i do believe it is important to keep the Sabbath as the Lord’s Day and to worship on the first day of the week, when Christ rose. As the pattern laid out in scripture. I do not think we have to literally rest from all worldly matters to keep this. As humans, we are to set aside a day of the week to worship and rest in the Lord. I think the proper way to observe the Sabbath day is to gather together as the body and worship our God and King. (Matt. 28, Acts 2) I do think it is important to set aside the Lord’s Day to be purposeful in your reflection on God and gather with the body in your local church. This way you hear the Word proclaimed, pray/reflect on the week. And partake of the Lord’s Supper in order to nourish your faith. Though I do not think per the confession, you can do nothing on the Sabbath. I do think doing things with your family, recreation, yardwork, etc. can all be means of rest and bring glory to God. This is part of our living our lives daily as Christians seeking to honor and serve Christ daily. In other words, as Christians we shouldn’t only think about Christ and his word on Sunday. Rather we are to live our daily lives praising him and serving him daily in our various callings. The Sabbath is the day set aside to prepare to live out this calling.” The second difference reads: “I have perceived difference with WCF chapter 4 section 1: “It pleased God the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, and for the manifestation of the glory of his eternal power, wisdom, and goodness in the beginning, to create, or make of nothing, the world, and all things therein whether visible or invisible, in the space of six days; and all very good.” My view of creation would be the standard day age view. The ‘six days’ are understood in the same sense as “in that day” of Isaiah 11:10-11 —that is, as periods of indefinite length and not necessarily of 24 hours duration. There are other similar uses of the Hebrew word for “day” (yôm) in Scripture to support this view of periods longer than 24 hours including that in the very context of Genesis 2:4.
