<article class="home-result home-result--rpr rpr-card" data-result-item data-result-url="exc/platte-valley__016.html" data-result-title="Platte Valley — 52nd (2025) RPR exception" data-result-type="RPR exception"><span class="home-result__metadata"><span class="home-result__category">RPR exception</span><span class="home-result__separator" aria-hidden="true">•</span><span>52nd (2025)</span></span><a class="home-result__title" data-result-primary data-result-type="RPR exception" data-result-title="BCO 21-4.f, BCO 21-4.g" href="exc/platte-valley__016.html">BCO 21-4.f, BCO 21-4.g</a><p class="rpr-card__body">Presbytery appears to have incorrectly ruled the following stated difference as merely semantic. Candidate appears to deny the spiritual presence of Christ in the Lord’s Supper, holding only to a memorialist view of the sacrament, which is hostile to our system of doctrine. In candidate’s own words: “I do affirm that by the Spirit, Christ is present in and at the observance of the Supper. I believe that the Spirit does confirm and encourage the faith of those who receive the elements by faith in following Jesus in a unique way. We are to remember with love for Christ and sorrow for our sins his body and blood which were sacrificed for us on the cross. But I cannot say that we feed on his spiritual blood or spiritual body in some literal fashion. The act, both physically and spiritually, occurred once and for all time at Golgotha. We rightly counter Roman Catholics who seem repeatedly to crucify Christ when practicing transubstantiation that turns the bread and wine into the corporeal body and blood to feed upon. I am uncomfortable with the Reformed rite if it turns the bread and wine into his spiritual body and spiritual blood upon which we literally feed. The RCC bodily crucifies Christ again and again through the Mass—or so it appears to many Protestants, myself included. I don’t wish to appear to crucify Christ spiritually again and again through a Lord’s Supper that has recipients eating the spiritual body and spiritual blood. When we “feed on him in our hearts by faith with thanksgiving,” I interpret this to mean that we pause to remember and celebrate that at the cross it was finished both in body and in spirit for our salvation—hallelujah and amen. We figuratively feed upon the accomplished work of Christ at the cross as we apply again and again its saving graces to our daily lives through the agency of the supper when accompanied by faith through the Holy Spirit. Again, I may be misunderstanding what the article is saying, in which case, I welcome conversation, and this might not be an exception. For more see the Larger Catechism 168-170 and the Shorter Catechism 96.”.</p><span class="home-result__facts"><span class="home-result__fact"><b>Provision(s):</b> BCO 21-4.f, BCO 21-4.g</span><span class="home-result__fact"><b>Lifecycle:</b> raised (52nd)</span><span class="home-result__fact"><b>Final disposition:</b> raised (open)</span></span><details class="result-actions"><summary>Actions</summary><div class="result-actions__panel" aria-label="Record actions"><button type="button" data-result-action="save">Save</button><button type="button" data-result-action="cite">Cite</button><button type="button" data-result-action="link">Copy link</button></div></details></article>
