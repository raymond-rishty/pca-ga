# GA47 O6 — Amend RAO 11 and 13 to Disallow Memorial Resolutions

**Assembly:** 47th (2019)  ·  **Source:** Pacific Northwest Presbytery (to CCB, OC) “Amend RAO 11 and 13 to Disallow Memorials”  ·  **Disposition:** Adopted (final)

*Source: [ga47_2019 p. 596](../markdown/ga47_2019.md#ga47-p596)*

---

## Submitted overture

(All parts should be considered as a single proposal, considered together.)

1. Add new RAO 11-12.

11-12. No communication or overture shall be received that seeks to memorialize someone.

2. Revise RAO 13-2 by adding new final sentence.

13-2. New business must be presented to the General Assembly before the recess of the afternoon session on the second day of business, and if it was not first presented as an overture to a Presbytery, the proposal must include an explanation for why it was not. If received by a two-thirds (2/3) vote of the commissioners present and voting, [it] shall be 594

referred by the Stated Clerk to the appropriate committee of commissioners. No proposed amendment to the Constitution shall be received as new business. No proposal that seeks to memorialize someone shall be received as new business.

3. Strike all of RAO 13-6.

<del>13-6. Any overture that deals with a memorial resolution</del> <del>which honors a recently deceased Teaching Elder or Ruling</del> <del>Elder and is received by the Stated Clerk within thirty days of</del> <del>the convening of a General Assembly shall be received by the</del> <del>Assembly and included by the Stated Clerk in the minutes of</del> <del>the General Assembly, if the overture 1) has been approved by</del> <del>a Presbytery, 2) memorializes an elder who has played a</del> <del>significant role in the General Assembly, and 3) is no longer</del> <del>than one thousand words. Ordinarily, memorial resolutions</del> <del>shall not be read to the Assembly</del>. Grounds:

1. There are problems with the current RAO 13-6, which became most apparent last year at the 2018 GA regarding Overtures 31, 36 and 37,

- **a)** There is currently no provision for the Assembly to consider approval/disapproval of a memorial, should it wish to do so.

- **b)** RAO currently has no provision for amending any resolution in a memorial overture.

- **c)** RAO currently does not restrict content. So, for example, it could allow memorial resolutions with differing views of the person and his contribution to the General Assembly.

- **d)** Currently, the text of any memorial overture would appear in an appendix of the Assembly Minutes, because its entirety would be included along with other Overtures. But a reader might be confused as to whether and how the Assembly acted on the memorial. Furthermore, if a memorial was in order per RAO 13-6 and thus "included by the Stated Clerk in the minutes of the General Assembly" (which would be a second inclusion), it would add to the confusion.

2. John Calvin was buried on Sunday, May 28, 1564, in an unmarked grave (per his request), at a secret location in Geneva. Earlier, Calvin had written the following about the death of Moses:

595

"It is good that famous men should be buried in unmarked graves." Commentaries on the Four Last Books of Moses, Vol. 4, 406 (Grand Rapids: Baker, 1979), on Deut. 34:6: “So Moses the servant of the Lord died there in the land of Moab, according to the word of the Lord, and he buried him in the valley in the land of Moab opposite Beth-peor; but no one knows the place of his burial to this day." (ESV)

Adopted by Pacific Northwest Presbytery at its stated meeting, January 25, 2019 Attested by /s/ RE Brett Chase, stated clerk
