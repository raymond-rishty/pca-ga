# GA47 O37 — Adopt an Assembly Statement on Same-Sex Attraction

**Assembly:** 47th (2019)  ·  **Source:** James River Presbytery “Assembly Statement on Same-sex Attraction”  ·  **Disposition:** Answered by reference to Overture 4

*Source: [ga47_2019 p. 689](../markdown/ga47_2019.md#ga47-p689)*

---

## Submitted overture

Whereas, there is tremendous cultural pressure in the United States in these days concerning homosexuality and sexual sin; and

Whereas, there are many people inside and outside the church who struggle with same-sex attraction and gender confusion; and

Whereas, the Revoice organization held a conference in 2018 which discussed how to improve the way the church ministers to people who struggle with same-sex attraction, encouraging them to uphold the Biblical sexual ethic; and

Whereas, we acknowledge and affirm that Revoice showed love to people struggling with these sins; and

Whereas, we acknowledge and affirm that the Church has not always shown love to people who struggle with these sins; and

(to OC)

Whereas, the Presbyterian Church in America, as part of its duty under the Great Commission, desires to better minister to people who struggle with same-sex attraction;

Whereas, the Church commends the faithfulness of Christians struggling with same-sex attraction who are seeking to remain faithful to a Biblical sexual ethic; Therefore, the 47th General Assembly of the Presbyterian Church in America affirms that:

1. That all persons are born sinners justly deserving God's wrath and condemnation and inherit both the guilt of our first parents as well as corruption. (Rom 5:12)

2. That outside of Christ, men and women are enslaved to corruption which enslaves the thinking of all persons and distorts God's created order. (Eph 2:1-8) That Christ has purchased not only the faith of his saints on the cross by which they are made just in God's sight but has also broken the power of sin/corruption to enslave His sheep. (Rom 8:1-11)

4. That Christ unites sinners to Himself, though they be corrupt through and through, but he does not leave them in corruption but subdues their corruption and produces holiness in them. (Rom 5:12-22, 6:1- 10; Col. 2:15)

5. That corruption draws all toward sinful desires whether they be anger, disobedience, pride, hatred, lusts, or unnatural attractions. (Eph 2:1-3).

6. That the Scriptures consistently exhort believers to reckon themselves dead to sin/corruption and alive to Christ (Rom 6:5-11, 1 Cor 6:9-11)

7. That “sexual orientation” is a modern invention describing men and women as being on a spectrum of sexual attraction or desire, and is not a Biblical category.

8. That the use of terms such as “Gay Christian,” “Same Sex Attracted Christian,” “Alcoholic Christian,” “Lustful Christian,” “Greedy Christian," or any other corrupt desire is not consistent with our union with Christ. Such descriptors of our fallenness must not be used as an identity marker but must rather be humbly submitted to Christ who provides our primary identity as Christians. (1 Cor. 6:9-11)

9. That the pull toward same-sex lust is not morally neutral but arises from our fallen nature, and with all other temptations must be mortified by the power which Christ supplies. (Rom. 1:18-27)

10. That Christians will struggle all their lives with sin and that the Church should never promise healing from sin in this life but the 688

Spirit's strength to kill unholy desires and to grow holy desires. (Rom 8:9-11)

11. That no temptation has overtaken any of us except that which is common to man and the Church must, therefore, be a place where we encourage one another in the battle against indwelling sin. (1 Cor 10:13)

12. That while there is a place for conferences and support groups, the Church is a haven for sinners and we should find solidarity in this common battle with sin not only with others who struggle exactly the same but primarily among those in our local congregation who share a common Savior. (Hebrews 10:25, Gal. 6:10)

Adopted by James River Presbytery at its stated meeting, April 13, 2019 Attested by /s/ RE Jeremy L. Pryor, stated clerk

<h3 class="overture-action-heading">Minority Report proposed 13-point substitute text for Overture 37</h3>

<blockquote class="overture-action-text">
We the undersigned recommend that the 47th GA affirm Overture 37 as amended below, affirming that:<br>
<br>
1. All human beings are created in God’s image, endowed with dignity and purpose to the glory of their Maker (Gen 1:26–27, 9:6; James 3:9).<br>
<br>
2. Some Christians and churches regrettably have not loved, listened to, or cared for those experiencing same sex attractions or gender confusion in a manner befitting their identity as God’s image bearers or, in the case of believers, as our brothers and sisters in Christ; and we exhort such Christians and churches to repent of these failures.<br>
<br>
3. All human beings descending from Adam by ordinary generation are born sinners, having inherited both imputed guilt and enslaving corruption from Adam, and justly deserve God’s judgment and wrath (Rom 5:12; Eph 2:1-3).<br>
<br>
4. By his death and resurrection Christ has not only procured the justification of his saints whereby they are forgiven and declared righteous in God’s sight, but has also secured their freedom from enslavement to sin so that they are able to die unto sin and live unto righteousness in Him (Rom 5:12–19, 6:1-14; Col 3:5-11).<br>
<br>
5. When the Spirit unites sinners to Christ by faith, Christ pardons and subdues the corruption of their nature by his grace and grows them in holiness as they work out their salvation with fear and trembling (Phil 2:12–13).<br>
<br>
6. Even after such union with Christ, there remains a corruption of their nature that continues to generate all manner of sinful desires, such as anger, disobedience, pride, hatred, lusts, or unnatural sexual attractions (Eph 2:1-3).<br>
<br>
7. While external temptations from Satan and the world are not sinful for those subjected to them, internal desires that arise from the corruption of the flesh, including romantic or sexual attraction to a member of the same sex, are sinful and must be pardoned and mortified in Christ (Heb 4:13; John 3:6).<br>
<br>
8. The notion that unnatural sexual desires, due to the fall, are merely “disordered” but not sinful, conflicts with the biblical and Reformed doctrine that both the corruption of our nature itself, and all the motions thereof, are truly and properly sin (Rom 7:5-8, 25; Gal 5:17; WCF 6.5).<br>
<br>
9. Terms such as “gay Christian,” “same-sex attracted Christian,” “alcoholic Christian,” “lustful Christian,” “greedy Christian,” etc., do not rightly define the identity of believers; rather, Christians must consider themselves dead to sin and alive to God in Christ, in whom they find their identity (Rom 6:11; 1 Cor. 6:9-11).<br>
<br>
10. Sanctification is not fully realized in this life, and the remnants of corruption remain in every part of the Christian, which remnants prompt an irreconcilable war between the flesh and the Spirit, such that the Church should never promise the complete removal of sinful desires, including same sex attraction (1 John 1:8; James 3:2; Gal 5:17).<br>
<br>
11. Same-sex attraction, spontaneously arising from a Christian’s remaining corruption, differs from sin in which a desire is not only conceived in the heart, but breaks forth into action, and those Christians who resist and refuse to act upon such spontaneous attractions in word, thought, and deed are actively mortifying sin in obedience to Christ (1 Cor 10:13; Col 3:5; WCF 6.5; WLC 151).<br>
<br>
12. The progressive character of sanctification in this life should lead the church to welcome, love, and care for suffering sinners, including those who struggle with same sex attraction and gender confusion, tenderly shepherding them to rest in Christ and walk increasingly in newness of life (1 Thess 5:14; 2 Tim 4:2; Rom 6:4).<br>
<br>
13. No temptation has overtaken anyone that is not common to man, so the Church must avoid being a place where those who struggle with same sex attraction and gender confusion are alienated, but rather serve as a haven for sinners who are loved and encouraged to battle against indwelling sin in the covenant community (1 Cor 10:13; Heb 10:25).
</blockquote>
