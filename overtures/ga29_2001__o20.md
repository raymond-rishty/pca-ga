# GA29 O20 — Clarify Presbytery Authority to Allow Exceptions on Creation Day Length

**Assembly:** 29th (2001)  ·  **Source:** New River Presbytery (to B&O & CCB) "Allow Exceptions on Length of Creation Days"

*Source: [ga29_2001 p. 198](../markdown/ga29_2001.md#ga29-p198)*

---

## Submitted overture

“Allow Exceptions on Length of Creation Days”

Whereas, the 28th General Assembly rejected the unanimous recommendation of the Creation Study Committee to wait two years before taking any action on the subject of the interpretation of the length of the days of creation in Genesis 1 and 2; and

Whereas, the 28th General Assembly gave approval to an action which, while described by the Moderator as simply ‘pious advice’, is being perceived by many in the denomination as pressure to approve candidates for office without regard to their views on these issues, therefore, BE IT

RESOLVED, that New River Presbytery, voting at its 80th Stated Meeting in Roanoke, Virginia on November 10, 2000, hereby encourages and requests the 29th General Assembly of the PCA to take the following actions as additions and further explanation of the Assembly’s response to the Creation Study Committee report: A. While the PCA recognizes both a historic and current diversity of views in the area of the length of creation days described in Genesis 1 and 2, it remains the primary and original responsibility of Presbyteries and Sessions to examine their candidates and determine if their views, no matter how diverse, are in accord with the Constitution of the church, and to determine if such views are exceptions. B. Presbyteries and Sessions who define any other view than the 24 hour day view as an exception to the standards in this area, in keeping with the actions of the 14th General Assembly, Section 143-52, Report of the Committee of Commissioners on Judicial Business, are to make the determination as to whether the teaching of these defined exceptions may or may not be allowed. C. Presbyteries and Sessions are counseled that, until such time as a view other than six, 24-hour solar day creation is adjudged to be out of accord with the fundamentals of the system of doctrine of the church through a change to the constitution, a final action in a judicial case, or the approval of a recommendation by a study committee, no officer should be refused ordination based on that exception alone. Adopted by New River Presbytery on November 10, 2000, at its 80th Stated Meeting. Attested by: /s/ Don K. Clements, Stated Clerk

<h3 class="overture-action-heading">CCB advice on overtures concerning the Creation Study Committee report (minutes excerpt)</h3>

<blockquote class="overture-action-text">
Item 1: Advice to the Stated Clerk in the matter of the role of in thesi statements. Since the adoption of the Creation Study Committee report as amended last year, I have received inquires as to the status of the report in relation to the constitution. Moreover, there are overtures this year to require exceptions for any views other than 24-hour creation days (Overture 7, 23) to allow exceptions for other view (Overture 20), and to affirm the Position of the 28th General Assembly on diversity of views on creation days (Overture 30).<br>
<br>
My advice has been as follows:<br>
<br>
- **a.** The Creation Study Committee Report has the status of any other study committee report, such as those on the gifts of the Holy Spirit and Freemasonry, that is, it is the thinking of a particular General Assembly on a specific subject.<br>
<br>
- **b.** The Creation Study Committee Report, as with all other study committee reports adopted by a General Assembly, does not amend the constitution (BCO Preface, III. &quot;The Constitution Defined&quot; and BCO 26-1); only amendments effected by the proper constitutional procedures are actual and lawful amendments (BCO 26-2, 26-3 and 26-6).<br>
<br>
- **C.** The Creation Study Committee Report has the status of being among those decisions of the General Assembly that are to be given &quot;due and serious consideration by the Church and its lower courts when deliberating matters related to such actions&quot; (BCO 14-7).<br>
<br>
- **d.** Overtures relating to the Creation Study Committee Report would have the same status as the report, i.e. of being among those decisions of the General Assembly that are to be given &quot;due and serious consideration by the Church and its lower courts when deliberating matters related to such actions&quot; (BCO 14-7). Response: The CCB ratifies the advice of the Stated Clerk relative to the Role of in thesi Statements dated April 21, 2001, and offers the following additional advice: It is the purview of the lower courts to determine the suitability of men applying for reception/ordination with regard to the men&#x27;s views on the matters raised in in thesi statements, subject always to proper review and/or judicial process. (BCO 13-6 and 13-9.a) (BCO 24-1) (BCO 11-3,4) Adopted by CCB
</blockquote>
