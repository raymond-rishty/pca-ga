# GA30 O32 — Amend BCO 21-4 to Define Good Faith Subscription and Allowable Exceptions

**Assembly:** 30th (2002)  ·  **Source:** Philadelphia Presbytery (to CCB & B&O) "Specify Good Faith Subscription – Version 7"

*Source: [ga30_2002 p. 236](../markdown/ga30_2002.md#ga30-p236)*

---

## Submitted overture

“Specify Good Faith Subscription - Version 7”

Whereas, the purity and unity of our church require her to establish that her officers sincerely receive and adopt the Westminster Standards as containing the system of doctrine taught in the Holy Scriptures, and for those officers to declare if at any time they find themselves out of accord with any of the fundamentals of this system {BCO 21-5; WCF XXXI.2); and

Whereas, the purity and unity of the church also require that her leaders understand both the restrictions and allowances of her Standards that are derived from Scripture but, unlike it, are not the only infallible rule of faith and practice {WCF 1.8, 10; XX.2; XXXI.3; WLC # 3; BCO Preface II. 7); and

Whereas, there is a longstanding tradition within American Presbyterianism, including the PCA, that officers may in good faith take exception to certain particulars of the Westminster Standards, if such particular exceptions are not inimical (i.e. hostile or injurious) to the more comprehensive system of doctrine; and

Whereas, the PCA’s original Good-faith subscription position has served the church well, but recently been challenged by some who desire either a more Broad- or Strict-subscription position; and

Whereas, the church must guard against a Broad substance of doctrine subscription approach, which has no definitive meaning and leaves to the candidate himself rather than to the wisdom of the ecclesiastical court to determine what areas of his disagreement with particular statements of the Constitution are inimical to the system of doctrine and what are allowable differences with the Westminster Standards; and

Whereas, the assertion that the PCA was consciously founded as a Strict-subscriptionist Church in 1973 cannot be sustained {Minutes of the Twenty-First General Assembly, cf. pp. 89, 166-168); and

Whereas, the General Assembly of the PCA did not declare itself to be a Strict-subscriptionist Church in the joining and receiving process with the Reformed Presbyterian Church Evangelical Synod in 1982; and

Whereas, attempts to change the PCA into a Broad- or Strict- subscriptionist Church are a departure from the original position of the PCA, and such attempts may bring discord and possibly division within the Church; and

Whereas, the inclusion of a Good-faith subscription statement in our Book of Church Order is needed to state explicitly what has been the understanding and practice of the majority of the PCA from its beginning; Therefore, to clarify and support the original ordination standards of the PCA, Philadelphia Presbytery overtures the General Assembly to amend the Book of Church Order by inserting the paragraph in bold below as new paragraphs of BCO 21-4 between the following sentences, as indicated: “Whenever a Presbytery shall omit any of these parts, it shall always make a record of the reasons for such omissions and of the trial parts omitted. [Insert new paragraphs here] “The Presbytery being fully satisfied of his qualifications for the sacred office....” Therefore, in examining a candidate for ordination the Presbytery shall inquire not only into the candidate’s knowledge and views in the areas specified above, but also shall require the candidate to state the specific instances in which he may differ with the confession of faith and Catechisms in any of their statements and/or propositions. The court may only grant an exception to any difference of doctrine if in the court’s judgment the candidate’s declared difference is out of accord with no fundamental of our system of doctrine because the difference is neither hostile to the system nor strikes at the vitals of religion. Minority Report (Overtures 10,3,11, 24,29,31,32) We recommend that Overture 10 from Ohio Valley Presbytery be answered as follows; and that Overture 3 from Illiana Presbytery, et al., Overture 11 from North Georgia Presbytery, et al., Overture 12 from Metropolitan Presbytery New York and Southern Florida Presbyteries, Overture 24 from North Texas Presbytery, Overture 29 from Westminster Presbytery, Overture 31 from Iowa Presbyteiy, and Overture 32 from Philadelphia Presbytery be answered by reference to the Assembly’s action on Overture 10: “That the General Assembly appoint an ad interim committee to bring recommendations on this issue, that the overtures on this matter be referred to the ad interim committee, that the committee report to the 31st General Assembly, that the budget of the committee be set at $10,000.00, and that the committee be funded by private donations.” [Offered as a substitutefor the majority report and defeated. See 30-47, p. 212.] Grounds: 1. 18 of 45 commissioners (40%) supported the above substitute motion, a significant minority which fact we believe ought to be before the General Assembly in their consideration of this matter. We further note that we who support an ad interim study committee represent a wide variety of views on subscription ourselves, but are united in our concern that the church handle this matter as wisely and peacefully as possible. (James 3:13,17- 18) 2. We agree with the majority that the matter of subscription is a significant issue that ought to be settled as definitively as possible and as soon as it is prudent for the peace, purity and unity of the church. We are thankful for the hard work and good motives of the many men and presbyteries that have so effectively brought this important matter before the church. (Ephesians 4:1-6; Philippians 1:3-6) 3. However, we also believe that this matter is arguably the most significant issue of polity to come before the General Assembly in its 30 year history, and thus demands the utmost level of consideration and care before its settlement. (Proverbs 19:2; 27:14) 4. We believe that Overture 10 deserves to be greatly improved by further study and refinement.
