## Submitted overture

"Require Exceptions for Views Other Than Six 24-Hour Creation Days"

Whereas, the Presbyterian Church in America is a confessional church based upon the Westminster Standards; and

Whereas, our Confession of Faith states the time frame of God's original creative activity with the phrase, "in the space of six days" (WCF 4:1); and

Whereas, growing historical evidence demonstrates the intent of the Westminster Assembly was to define the six creation days as 24-hour calendar days (At least 13 Westminster divines have been identified as attesting to a 24-hour creation day in their writings and recorded sermons; while none has been documented as holding to other time frames.); and

Whereas, the official position of Calvary Presbytery states that the doctrine of the Confessional standards of the Presbyterian Church in America explicitly asserts that the world was created by divine fiat, literally “in the space of six days”. Because the Confession’s teaching on creation is unassailably grounded in Scripture, we joyfully proclaim that our preaching and teaching should conform to its content and emphases. (Adopted October 24, 1995); and

Whereas, the 28th General Assembly affirmed that “...a diversity of views on creation days...is acceptable” thus, in essence, invalidating any definitive creedal statement; and

Whereas, it has been our historic practice during elder examinations to note any differences from our standards as exceptions to be discussed by the church court; Therefore Be It Resolved, that the 29th General Assembly of the PCA declare that, with charity toward all our members, and with fidelity to the original authors of our confessional standards, and until evidence to the contrary is found, we understand the Westminster divines intended the phrase “in the space of six days” to specify that the six days of creation were days of normal duration with evening and morning; and Be It Further Resolved, that, for the peace of the church, any future candidates who differ with this original meaning should request an exception to the sense of the Confession unless documentation that the Westminster divines held other views is firmly established or unless the Standards are duly amended; and Be It Finally Resolved, that courts of original jurisdiction make these future determinations with care, charity, honesty, and impartiality. Adopted by Calvary Presbytery on January 27, 2001. Attested by: /s/ Charles E. Champion, Stated Clerk

<h3 class="overture-action-heading">CCB advice on overtures concerning the Creation Study Committee report (minutes excerpt)</h3>

<blockquote class="overture-action-text">
Item 1: Advice to the Stated Clerk in the matter of the role of in thesi statements. Since the adoption of the Creation Study Committee report as amended last year, I have received inquires as to the status of the report in relation to the constitution. Moreover, there are overtures this year to require exceptions for any views other than 24-hour creation days (Overture 7, 23) to allow exceptions for other view (Overture 20), and to affirm the Position of the 28th General Assembly on diversity of views on creation days (Overture 30).<br>
<br>
My advice has been as follows:<br>
<br>
- **a.** The Creation Study Committee Report has the status of any other study committee report, such as those on the gifts of the Holy Spirit and Freemasonry, that is, it is the thinking of a particular General Assembly on a specific subject.<br>
<br>
- **b.** The Creation Study Committee Report, as with all other study committee reports adopted by a General Assembly, does not amend the constitution (BCO Preface, III. &quot;The Constitution Defined&quot; and BCO 26-1); only amendments effected by the proper constitutional procedures are actual and lawful amendments (BCO 26-2, 26-3 and 26-6).<br>
<br>
- **C.** The Creation Study Committee Report has the status of being among those decisions of the General Assembly that are to be given &quot;due and serious consideration by the Church and its lower courts when deliberating matters related to such actions&quot; (BCO 14-7).<br>
<br>
- **d.** Overtures relating to the Creation Study Committee Report would have the same status as the report, i.e. of being among those decisions of the General Assembly that are to be given &quot;due and serious consideration by the Church and its lower courts when deliberating matters related to such actions&quot; (BCO 14-7). Response: The CCB ratifies the advice of the Stated Clerk relative to the Role of in thesi Statements dated April 21, 2001, and offers the following additional advice: It is the purview of the lower courts to determine the suitability of men applying for reception/ordination with regard to the men&#x27;s views on the matters raised in in thesi statements, subject always to proper review and/or judicial process. (BCO 13-6 and 13-9.a) (BCO 24-1) (BCO 11-3,4) Adopted by CCB
</blockquote>
