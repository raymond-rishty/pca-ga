# GA47 O27 — Study the Possibility of Remote Voting at General Assembly

**Assembly:** 47th (2019)  ·  **Source:** Westminster Presbytery  ·  **Disposition:** Answered in the negative

*Source: [ga47_2019 p. 659](../markdown/ga47_2019.md#ga47-p659)*

---

## Submitted overture

(to AC)
“Study Possibility of Remote Voting at General Assembly"

Whereas, the Presbyterian Church in America was founded as a "grass-
roots" denomination with power and authority flowing from the bottom
up; and

Whereas, the expense of sending a commissioner to the General Assembly
has become prohibitive for many small churches in the PCA; and

Whereas, many commissioners would attend the General Assembly, yet are
unable because of health restrictions; and

Whereas, the General Assembly meetings now have a small representation
by ruling elders; and

Whereas, it is in accordance with the founding principles of the PCA to have
parity of the teaching and ruling elders in authority; and

Whereas, it is in accordance with the founding principles of the PCA and
our BCO to have parity of teaching and ruling elders on General
Assembly committees (BCO 14-1; Principles for the Organization of the
Assembly, item 10) and to have at least parity of teaching and ruling
elders at General Assembly court meetings (BCO 14-2); and

Whereas, the technology exists to have commissioners vote on issues of the
General Assembly from a distance while they watch the proceedings of
the General Assembly online; and

Whereas, the General Assembly as presently structured would continue with
the exception of a distance voting commissioner;
Therefore, Westminster Presbytery overtures the 47th General Assembly to
instruct the PCA Administrative Committee to investigate and report at
the 48th General Assembly a plan to create a DVC (Distance Voting
Commissioner) which would entail the ability of regularly elected
commissioners to vote electronically at a distance from the site of the
General Assembly.
Adopted by Westminster Presbytery at its stated meeting, March 9, 2019
Attested by /s/ TE Mark J. Blalack, stated clerk

<h3 class="overture-action-heading">GA47 AC Committee of Commissioners recommends a negative answer</h3>

<blockquote class="overture-action-text">
4. That Overture 27 from Westminster Presbytery, &quot;Study Possibility of Remote Voting at General Assembly,&quot; be answered in the negative. <del>with the proviso that the AC report its finding and</del> <del>positive or negative recommendation regarding the feasibility to the</del> <del>Forty eighth General Assembly in 2020</del>. Adopted Grounds: Both creation and redemption highlight the value of humans being embodied creatures. The historic practice at GA of singing Psalm 133 with its distinctively physical images, as well as RAO 1-1 which indicates that the GA begin with a worship service including prayer, a sermon, and the Lord&#x27;s Supper, recognize the church&#x27;s physical presence together in one place as being constitutive of the Assembly. Because a technology exists does not mean it should be implemented. As indicated by our cultural moment, conversation on weighty matters involving deeply held theological and religious convictions are best discussed face to face. Engaging important issues in the same room is fundamentally different than over a screen. Implementing Distance Voting runs the risk of communicating to TEs and REs (and to congregants) that being physical present for the work of the church is not essential. As indicated in the Theological Ground, Christian worship is physical in nature. The work of the General Assembly and our church courts goes beyond voting on substantive issues, but glorifying God and edifying one another as the body of Christ. The primary concern of Overture 27 is the involvement of REs in the work of the GA. However, no supportive data is given that indicates that proxy voting would increase RE involvement. The overture does not have a specific cost and cap of implementing a new technology. Currently the AC does not have appropriate cyber security policies in place for implementation of a distributive service (i.e. cloud service).
</blockquote>
