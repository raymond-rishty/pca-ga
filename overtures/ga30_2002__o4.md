# GA30 O4 — Amend BCO 21-4 to Require Recording of Presbytery-Allowed Exceptions to Standards

**Assembly:** 30th (2002)  ·  **Source:** Ohio Valley Presbytery (to CCB & B&O) "Handling Exceptions to the Westminster Standards"

*Source: [ga30_2002 p. 240](../markdown/ga30_2002.md#ga30-p240)*

---

## Submitted overture

“Handling Exceptions to the Westminster Standards” Add two new paragraphs in BCO 21-4 where deemed appropriate by the Committee on Constitutional Business: “When Presbytery allows a man to hold an exception to the standards, it shall also determine the man may hold the exception in belief only, whether he may teach it, and whether he may practice it either in his private life or in his public ministry all so long as he does not disturb the peace and purity of the church.” “The Stated Clerk of Presbytery shall cause the man’s allowed exception(s) stated in his own words, along with the Presbytery’s action on the exception(s), to be recorded along with the ministerial obligation he is required to sign. This document will be attached to the minutes of Presbytery as an appendix.” 4. That the General Assembly allow the Committee to give a partial report on Overture 17 from Eastern Canada Presbytery early in the Assembly. Adopted Grounds: Overture 17 [see text below in Recommendation 5] proposes a change in the RAO. Even though we are recommending that the Overture be answered in the negative, we believe the Overture and our recommendation should be presented early in the Assembly so the Overture has a fair chance of being considered under RAO XVIII.

## Action history

<h3 class="overture-action-heading">GA30 CCB finds constitutional conflicts and ambiguity in Overture 4</h3>

<blockquote class="overture-action-text">
Overture 4 from Ohio Valley Presbytery: &quot;Handling Exceptions to the Westminster Standards&quot; It is the opinion of the CCB that the proposed constitutional language creates constitutional conflicts with the BCO Preliminary Principles. Furthermore, the proposed language creates constitutional ambiguity.<br>
<br>
1. The proposed overture language &quot;either in his private life&quot; is in conflict with BCO Preliminary Principle II.1 and II.7.<br>
<br>
2. The proposed overture language requiring Presbyteries to record every man&#x27;s views disagreeing with the Constitution will erode the Presbyteries&#x27; exclusive authority to determine if a candidate receives and adopts the Confession of Faith and the Catechism of the PCA as containing the system of doctrine taught in the Holy Scriptures. See BCO 21-5. This is in conflict with BCO Preliminary Principle II.2 that recognizes the right of each court to determine its membership. (BCO Preface II.2, BCO 21-1 through 7; BCO 13-1; BCO 13-9 a, c, d; BCO 11-3 &amp; 4).<br>
<br>
3. An ambiguity created by the proposed language is the introduction of the phrase: &quot;exception to the standards.&quot; This phrase is nowhere else found in our constitution. The phrase elicits a plethora of interpretations, many of which go beyond our present Constitutional parameters.<br>
<br>
4. It has not normally been the mission of the CCB to decide on the placement of Constitutional language. [For text and action, see 30-50, III, 3, p. 238.] Adopted by CCB
</blockquote>
