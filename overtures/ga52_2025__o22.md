# GA52 O22 — Amend BCO 20-3, 24-3, and 25-1 to Permit Congregational Minimum Voting Age

**Assembly:** 52nd (2025)  ·  **Source:** the Pacific Northwest Presbytery (to CCB, OC) “Amend BCO 20-3, 24-3 and 25-1 to clarify "R  ·  **Disposition:** Answered in the negative

*Source: [ga52_2025 p. 1208](../markdown/ga52_2025.md#ga52-p1208)*

## Submitted overture

- **A.**

Be it resolved that BCO 20-3, 24-3, and BCO 25-1 be amended as shown below. Proposed additions are indicated by underlining.

20-3. When a congregation is convened for the election of a pastor... All communing members in good and regular standing, but no others, are entitled to vote in the churches to which they are respectively attached. (cf. BCO 25-1)

24-3. All communing members in good and regular standing, but no others, are entitled to vote in the election of church officers in the churches to which they respectively belong. A majority vote of those present is required for election. (cf. BCO 25-1)

25-1. The congregation consists of all the communing members of a particular church, and they only are entitled to vote. A congregation

1206

may establish a rule setting a minimum voting age (regular standing), but it must be adopted by a two-thirds (2/3) majority at a congregational meeting that is called with at least 30 days' notice. (cf. BCO 20-3, 24-3)

- **B.** Be it also resolved that if GA adopts this, or an amended version, the Stated Clerk will make this full Rationale available when the Amendment Ballot is sent to Presbyteries.

Rationale - The need for this Overture arises from an SJC Decision in March 2024, where, by a vote of 15-5, the SJC ruled that the BCO does not permit a congregation to set a minimum voting age.7 This Overture seeks to permit such. It argues that such has historically been common in American Presbyterianism. It's part of what was, and is, meant by "regular" standing. The Psiaki Decision did not define what the word "regular" means in the phrase "good and regular standing" and this Overture seeks to treat it as it has historically been understood. When the PCA was formed in 1973 it adopted the PCUS BCO of 1925. Minor communicants did not vote in 1925, as they were not regarded as being in "regular" standing. Our BCO 25-1 and 24-3 remain unchanged from 1925. The sentence above in BCO 20-3 remains unchanged from the PCUS BCO of 1879.8

This Overture's rationale provides many and varied arguments for why the PCA should allow such freedom, which is generally described below in Robert's Rules.

Some organized societies define additional classes of "membership" that do not entail all of these rights. Whenever the term member is used in this book [RONR], it refers to full participating membership in the assembly, unless otherwise specified. Such members are also described as "voting members" when it is necessary to make a distinction. (RONR (12th ed.) 1:4)
