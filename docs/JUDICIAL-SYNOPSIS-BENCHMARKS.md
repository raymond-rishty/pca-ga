# Judicial synopsis benchmarks

These editorial examples are a comparison set for Issue #149, not replacements
for the case text or a bulk rewrite of the production summaries. Read a synopsis
with its case ID, title, matter type, and disposition visible beside it. Names
and locations belong in the caption unless they explain the decision.

Use the repository skill
[$pca-ga-case-synopses](../.agents/skills/pca-ga-case-synopses/SKILL.md)
for the reusable drafting, source-checking, and assessment workflow.

## What earns a place in the benchmark

A useful synopsis explains the concrete dispute, the decision, its decisive
reason, and the relief or next step. Its distinctive value may be a narrow
constitutional distinction, an evidentiary limitation, a procedural rule, or
the reason a requested remedy was unavailable. It need not claim historical
importance. A case's later influence requires separate evidence.

Score each dimension from 0 (missing or misleading) to 2 (specific and useful):

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Concrete dispute | Can a reader tell what conduct or court action was challenged? |
| Decision | Does it say which material issues succeeded or failed? |
| Decisive reason | Does it explain why the deciding court reached that result? |
| Distinctive value | Does it preserve the rule, distinction, limitation, or remedy worth finding this case for? |
| Fidelity | Are allegations, findings, adopted reasoning, separate opinions, and procedural history kept distinct? |
| Economy | Does each detail help explain the dispute, decision, or significance? |

Fidelity is a publication requirement, not a weakness that other points can
offset. A materially inaccurate holding fails regardless of the total. Aim for
60 to 90 words for a reasoned decision, with flexibility for complexity. A short
procedural record should remain short. Where the source gives no reason, score
the synopsis on accurately preserving that limit, not on supplying a reason.
Do not inflate a docket notice to achieve a word count or score.

The paragraphs below are the sample synopses. The separate **Benchmark lesson**
and **Evidence** notes are for editors, not part of the public-facing paragraph.
They identify what another summary must preserve. These are source-checked
editorial candidates, not declarations that every paragraph scores perfectly.

## Coverage and limits

The catalog inspected for this set contains 477 records, 63 observed
matter-type/individual-disposition pairs, and 107 distinct combinations when
the complete ordered disposition list is counted. Coverage below uses the 63
individual pairs; an example can cover several pairs, but its synopsis must
retain the complete result. This is not a claim to provide two examples for
every one of the 107 composite permutations.

Two examples are supplied where the source checks support them. A shared
decision or companion docket is not counted as two independent examples of
the same holding. The tables distinguish usable examples from singletons,
short-record examples, and suspect catalog combinations. An unfilled cell is
not evidence that such a proceeding is constitutionally impossible or has
never occurred.

Matter type follows the proceeding being decided, not a procedure mentioned
in its history. A complaint about original jurisdiction remains a complaint.
A complaint about a reference remains a complaint. An appeal's rejection does
not itself constitute a new guilty verdict.

## Complaint synopses

<a id="c01"></a>
### C01. 1978-02: Allin et al. v. Tennessee Valley Presbytery

Disposition: complaint sustained in part; remaining specification denied; remanded.

Session members challenged Presbytery's order to hold a congregational meeting on officer rotation, alleging inadequate notice and improper participation in the complaint hearing. The Judicial Commission held that the ten-day notice period ran from the postmark and that members of the Session under review could not participate as judges. It nevertheless upheld the congregation's right to a meeting upon a sufficient petition: ordering the meeting did not mandate rotation. The complaint was sustained in part and remanded for a properly noticed hearing.

Benchmark lesson: preserve both the procedural errors and the unsuccessful claim that Presbytery mandated rotation. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga06_1978__case2.md), explanatory opinion and judgment.

<a id="c02"></a>
### C02. 2022-22: Turner et al. v. South Florida Presbytery

Disposition: complaint sustained in part; findings, censures, and corrective mandates annulled; returned to Presbytery.

Presbytery investigated a Session's handling of sexual-abuse allegations and found it guilty of delinquencies without a trial. The SJC upheld the investigation but annulled the findings of guilt, censures, and mandated corrective actions. It held that credible reports could justify investigation even when submitted through procedurally defective complaints, while censure required formal judicial process or an admission of guilt. The matter was returned for Presbytery to pursue records review, informal resolution, or formal process under BCO 40-6.

Benchmark lesson: legitimate investigation and illegitimate censure can coexist. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga51_2024__2022-22.md), issues, judgment, and reasoning.

<a id="c03"></a>
### C03. 1982-05: Workman et al. v. Eastern Carolina Presbytery

Disposition: complaint sustained.

A Session challenged Presbytery's resolution describing total abstinence from alcoholic beverages as the proper Christian lifestyle and calling on officers and congregations to follow it. The Assembly sustained the complaint. Although the resolution described abstinence as voluntary, the Judicial Commission concluded that its form would in practice bind members' consciences beyond Scripture and the Westminster Confession. The decision distinguished the church's responsibility to oppose intemperance from imposing abstinence as its prescribed standard of Christian conduct.

Benchmark lesson: the word “voluntary” did not resolve the conscience issue. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga10_1982__case5.md), explanatory opinion, judgment, and appendage. The proceeding is a complaint, not a reference.

<a id="c04"></a>
### C04. 2002-16: Delhi Presbyterian Church Session v. Louisiana Presbytery

Disposition: complaint sustained; presbytery judgment declared a nullity.

Presbytery assumed jurisdiction over a church member and barred him from the Lord's Table for refusing to meet with its commission, although his Session had investigated the underlying allegations and declined to institute process. The SJC sustained the Session's complaint. An investigation ending without charges was not a refusal to act permitting Presbytery to assume jurisdiction under BCO 33-1. The resulting judgment was a nullity, and the member remained subject to his Session's jurisdiction and decisions.

Benchmark lesson: distinguish refusal to act from a decision not to prosecute. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga32_2004__2002-16.md), issues 1 to 3, judgment, and majority reasoning. This is not a BCO 34-1 request.

<a id="c05"></a>
### C05. 2008-11: Broadwater et al. v. Chesapeake Presbytery

Disposition: complaint sustained in part; original-jurisdiction mandate vacated; reference mandate left standing.

Presbytery empowered a commission both to assume original jurisdiction over matters involving church members and to receive a Session's reference about a membership transfer. The SJC vacated the original-jurisdiction portion because Presbytery had neither found that the Session refused to act nor required that finding before the commission proceeded. The commission's authority to receive the reference remained intact. Requests from other Sessions did not, by themselves, establish the jurisdictional conditions of BCO 33-1.

Benchmark lesson: identify the two mandates and which survived. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga38_2010__2008-11.md), judgment and reasoning for 2008-11, before the next case heading.

<a id="c06"></a>
### C06. 2007-02: Malone v. Metropolitan New York Presbytery

Disposition: complaint sustained in part; judgment and censure vacated without prejudice.

A minister challenged discipline imposed after an investigative commission treated his correspondence acknowledging patterns of sin as a confession authorizing judgment without process. The SJC sustained the complaint in part and vacated the judgment and censure. BCO 38-1 required a clear confession of specific offenses and a demonstrated intention to submit to judgment on that confession; expressions of regret were insufficient. Presbytery could proceed further, but had to begin its process anew.

Benchmark lesson: vacating an invalid confession procedure does not acquit the accused. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga36_2008__2007-02.md), judgment and reasoning on BCO 38-1.

<a id="c07"></a>
### C07. 2011-09: Jennings v. North Florida Presbytery

Disposition: complaint sustained; restoration actions annulled; remanded.

Presbytery restored a minister deposed for adultery by a vote of 19 to 17. The SJC sustained a complaint against the restoration, concluding that this closely divided vote did not demonstrate the strong general sentiment in favor of restoration required by BCO 34-8. It annulled Presbytery's actions and remanded for compliance with the restoration requirements. The decision concerns the showing needed for restoration, not a declaration that restoration after such a deposition is impossible.

Benchmark lesson: the vote is a material particular here, unlike names or geography. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga40_2012__2011-09.md), majority judgment and reasoning; the dissent disputes the majority's treatment of the vote.

<a id="c08"></a>
### C08. 2019-04: Williams v. Chesapeake Presbytery

Disposition: complaint sustained; approval of commission report annulled; matter returned.

After indicting a minister for sins relating to his marriage, a presbytery commission attempted to treat his partial admissions as a BCO 38-1 case without process. The SJC sustained his complaint and annulled Presbytery's approval of the commission's report. It held that a guilty plea within an instituted case and a confession initiating a case without process are distinct procedures. The commission could dismiss the case or resume process by receiving pleas to the indictment.

Benchmark lesson: explain the procedural distinction and the available next steps without implying acquittal. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga48_2021__2019-04.md), judgment and reasoning.

<a id="c09"></a>
### C09. 2008-09: Red Mountain Session v. Evangel Presbytery

Disposition: all four elements of complaint sustained; presbytery action reversed.

A husband sought a church ruling that his wife's pursuit of divorce was unbiblical. Presbytery faulted the Session's response and concluded that she lacked biblical grounds. The SJC sustained all four elements of the Session's complaint and reversed Presbytery. The Session's investigation disclosed no clear error, and Presbytery's indirect evidence did not justify judging the wife apart from due process. The SJC expressly declined to decide whether she actually had biblical grounds for divorce.

Benchmark lesson: do not turn reversal of an improper judgment into approval of the divorce. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga37_2009__2008-09.md), judgments and concluding reasoning. The catalog's “partially sustained” label conflicts with the explicit conclusion.

<a id="c10"></a>
### C10. 2002-11: Abshire v. Pacific Northwest Presbytery

Disposition: one issue sustained; other decided issues rejected; underlying complaint remanded.

A minister challenged Presbytery's appointment of a judicial commission, its authority and pastoral exhortation, and its immediate ruling on a complaint against him. The SJC rejected the challenges to the appointment, authority, and exhortation, but held that the commission had decided the complaint without following BCO 43's hearing procedures. That complaint was remanded to Presbytery. The successful procedural objection did not invalidate every action of the commission or resolve the underlying complaint's merits.

Benchmark lesson: replace “partial reversal” with the actual successful issue and remedy. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga31_2003__2002-11.md), issues, judgment, and reasoning on issue 4.

<a id="c11"></a>
### C11. 2009-05: Payne v. Western Carolina Presbytery

Disposition: complaint unsuccessful; investigation and no-strong-presumption finding upheld.

Complainants challenged both a presbytery investigation of accusations against a minister and its conclusion that no strong presumption of guilt existed, objecting particularly to delays and the failure to interview grievance writers. The SJC rejected the challenges. BCO 31-2 required diligence but gave the investigating court substantial procedural discretion, unlike the more prescribed procedures after process begins. The record did not establish clear error warranting interference with that discretion.

Benchmark lesson: distinguish investigation from trial and identify the discretion actually reviewed. Evidence: [combined decision](../cases/ga38_2010__2009-05_2009-08_2009-09_2009-10.md), issues and majority reasoning. Companion dockets are not additional independent examples.

<a id="c12"></a>
### C12. 2016-01: Aven v. Ohio Valley Presbytery

Disposition: complaint denied.

A complainant argued that Presbytery had failed to obey an SJC remand requiring it to hear further from a minister about his paedocommunion views. The SJC denied the complaint because the minister had supplied additional explanations, including affirmations and denials and biblical exegesis. The prior remand had prescribed no particular examination procedure. This decision addressed compliance with the instruction to supplement the record, not a fresh determination of the minister's doctrinal conformity.

Benchmark lesson: do not import the earlier case's remand as this case's disposition. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga45_2017__2016-01.md), judgment and majority reasoning.

<a id="c13"></a>
### C13. 1982-04: Hathaway et al. v. Grace Presbytery

Disposition: complaint not sustained, with directions to Western Carolinas Presbytery.

Complainants challenged a presbytery's refusal to request that the Assembly assume original jurisdiction over allegations against a minister in another presbytery. The Assembly did not sustain the complaint because the minister's presbytery had not refused jurisdiction. It nevertheless directed that presbytery to proceed at its next stated meeting and held that the one-year limitation did not bar the case, given its commission's undue delay in beginning formal process. The result was conditional, not an unqualified approval of the handling below.

Benchmark lesson: an unsuccessful complaint can still produce material directions. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga10_1982__case4.md), adopted recommendation and grounds.

<a id="c14"></a>
### C14. 1992-04: Conrad et al. v. Central Carolina Presbytery

Disposition: administratively out of order; remanded to Interim Session.

Complainants treated Presbytery's return of their complaints to a commission serving as an Interim Session as a failure to act. The SJC disagreed. In its sessional capacity, the commission was the court of original jurisdiction and should hear the complaints first. The SJC found the complaint administratively out of order and remanded for timely adjudication, preserving the complainants' ability to take an adverse decision to the next higher court.

Benchmark lesson: administrative rejection can accompany a remand without deciding the underlying grievances. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga20_1992__1992-04.md), disposition.

<a id="c15"></a>
### C15. 2019-14: Yoo et al. v. Korean Southwest Orange County Presbytery

Disposition: administratively out of order; dismissed.

The SJC dismissed a complaint because it received no documentation showing that the complaint had first been presented to the presbytery whose action was challenged. The submission also included charges and specifications that belonged before Presbytery. The ruling enforced the required initial forum under BCO 43-2; it did not decide whether the alleged errors or offenses had occurred.

Benchmark lesson: explain the missing procedural step without inventing the underlying controversy. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga48_2021__2019-14.md), adopted decision.

<a id="c16"></a>
### C16. 2016-07: Avery and Lewelling v. Nashville Presbytery

Disposition: judicially out of order; dismissed.

Complainants challenged a presbytery committee's initiation of an investigation of a minister and its forwarding of the report. The SJC dismissed the complaint as judicially out of order because BCO 43-1 reaches actions of church courts, not activities of their committees. To the extent the complaint challenged an earlier action of Presbytery itself, it was untimely. The ruling did not adjudicate the investigation's substantive allegations.

Benchmark lesson: retain both grounds and distinguish committee action from court action. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga45_2017__2016-07.md), adopted decision.

<a id="c17"></a>
### C17. 2022-20: Wilson et al. v. Pacific Northwest Presbytery

Disposition: judicially out of order; dismissed.

Members challenged a voting restriction imposed at a congregational meeting. The SJC dismissed the complaint because a congregation is not a church court whose actions are complainable under BCO 43-1. It identified other routes: members could report the alleged unconstitutional restriction to Presbytery, then complain against Presbytery's response, or seek review through the church records. The adopted decision resolved the procedural route, not the substantive validity of the voting restriction.

Benchmark lesson: do not present the separate opinions' fuller suffrage arguments as the adopted holding. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga50_2023__2022-20.md), decision preceding the concurring opinion.

<a id="c18"></a>
### C18. 2003-06: Wright v. Eastern Carolina Presbytery

Disposition: judicially out of order.

A ruling elder complained against Presbytery's response to a Session's request for advice on a doctrinal issue. The SJC held that the response was nonbinding advice rather than establishment of a doctrinal position and therefore was not a complainable action. It found the complaint judicially out of order. The dissent disputed the characterization of the response as merely advisory, but that argument was not the court's adopted rationale.

Benchmark lesson: a dispute about the nature of a reference can itself come before the SJC as a complaint. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga32_2004__2003-06.md), adopted decision and separately identified dissent.

<a id="c19"></a>
### C19. 2022-05: Crouse et al. v. Northwest Georgia Presbytery

Disposition: out of order and moot.

A complaint challenged judicial process against three ruling elders. Before the SJC decided it, the Session dismissed all charges and ended the process; the elders also resigned and their calls were dissolved at their request. The SJC held the complaint out of order and moot because the underlying dispute had been settled and the challenged process no longer remained. It did not decide the alleged procedural errors.

Benchmark lesson: explain the event that removed the dispute; the Session's dismissal of charges is not an SJC merits ruling. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga50_2023__2022-05.md), decision on complaint.

<a id="c20"></a>
### C20. 2016-13: Daniels et al. v. Nashville Presbytery

Disposition: complaint found moot. Short-record example.

The SJC held that circumstances had rendered the complaint moot. Its adopted ruling did not identify those circumstances or explain why they ended the dispute. The separate dissent challenged that lack of explanation. No merits determination or specific mootness rationale appears in the adopted ruling.

Benchmark lesson: an unexplained disposition must remain unexplained in an editorial synopsis. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga46_2018__2016-13.md), adopted decision before the dissent.

<a id="c21"></a>
### C21. 1997-14 and 1997-15: George v. Central Florida Presbytery

Disposition: abandoned. Short-record example; two dockets, one illustrative event.

The SJC deemed the complaints abandoned after the complainant died without heirs. The published entries supply no substantive reasoning or merits determination. The same explanation appears under both docket numbers.

Benchmark lesson: preserve the recorded reason for abandonment without treating it as voluntary withdrawal. Evidence: [case entries](../cases/ga26_1998__1997-14_1997-15.md).

<a id="c22"></a>
### C22. 1983-09: Village Chapel Session v. Eastern Carolina Presbytery

Disposition: complaint abandoned; separate reference advice confirmed.

The Judicial Commission deemed the Session's complaint abandoned after the complainant failed to appear despite notice and a two-day opportunity to do so, without a satisfactory explanation. Separately, the commission confirmed advice declining to answer questions properly addressed through complaint or review and control, while answering a congregational-meeting question by reference to BCO 25-2. Confirmation of that advice was not an affirmance of the abandoned complaint on its merits.

Benchmark lesson: keep separate procedural vehicles and their outcomes separate within a shared report. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga11_1983__case9.md), judgment sections A and B.

<a id="c23"></a>
### C23. 2022-14: Oh v. Korean Southeast Presbytery

Disposition: withdrawn. Short-record example.

The complainant withdrew his complaint on August 29, 2022. The SJC's published decision records that withdrawal without deciding the underlying grievance or giving a reason for the withdrawal.

Benchmark lesson: do not add a dismissal or speculate about settlement. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga50_2023__2022-14.md), decision on complaint.

<a id="c24"></a>
### C24. 2009-14: Ellisville Presbyterian Church Session v. Grace Presbytery

Disposition: withdrawn. Short-record example.

The SJC docket records the Session's complaint as withdrawn. The entry supplies neither a reason for withdrawal nor a decision on the underlying dispute.

Benchmark lesson: this is an accurate status synopsis, not a miniature merits opinion. Evidence: [docket excerpt](../cases/ga38_2010__stub_2009-14.md).

<a id="c25"></a>
### C25. 1989-12: First PCA of Rowlett Session v. North Texas Presbytery

Disposition: referred for adjudication; historical stage only.

A Session challenged Presbytery's reception of a minister who held that remarriage was impermissible for any divorced person. The Assembly's commission found the record incomplete and could not adjudicate the doctrinal complaint. To avoid delaying the matter for another year, it directed adjudication to the Standing Judicial Commission. This report records a transfer for decision, not a ruling approving or rejecting the minister's view.

Benchmark lesson: a referral synopsis explains why the case moved and what remained undecided at that stage. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga17_1989__case7.md), adjudication of Case #3. Do not present this historical stage as proof that no later decision exists.

<a id="c26"></a>
### C26. 1981-02: Zeigler v. Texas Presbytery

Disposition: lower-court judgment confirmed. Short-reasoning record.

Complainants objected to Presbytery's use of a committee rather than their requested commission to examine a complaint, and to its hearing other participants before meeting with them. The Assembly's Judicial Commission unanimously confirmed the lower court's judgment. The published adjudication gives no explanatory reasoning, so it does not establish a general rule approving every such committee arrangement or interview sequence.

Benchmark lesson: describe the dispute without converting an unexplained affirmance into a broad precedent. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga09_1981__case2.md), complaint and adjudication; the printed era identifier is Case 3.

<a id="c27"></a>
### C27. 1985-03: Watterson v. PCA

Disposition: not in order.

A complainant brought a matter to the General Assembly before Presbytery had acted on it and without first filing the complaint with the presbytery having jurisdiction. The Assembly found the complaint not in order under BCO 43-2 and 43-3. The decision addresses premature resort to the higher court rather than the substance of the grievance.

Benchmark lesson: do not infer a more specific out-of-order label than the minutes use. Evidence: [13th Assembly minutes](../markdown/ga13_1985.md), adopted recommendation for Case 3, item 13-47.

<a id="c28"></a>
### C28. 1989-01: Stine v. Southern Florida Presbytery

Disposition: neither specification sustained; Presbytery's decision confirmed.

A member challenged a church-sponsored Sunday activity that combined prayer, singing, and exhortation with games involving adults and children, alleging violations of Sabbath observance and ordination vows. The Assembly's commission rejected both specifications and confirmed Presbytery's decision. It also urged Sessions to consider carefully the activities they sponsor on the Lord's Day. The adopted actions give no fuller explanation of why this activity was permissible; the dissent argued that it violated the church's confessional commitments.

Benchmark lesson: retain the specific activity and limited published explanation without inventing a general Sabbath-recreation rule. Evidence: [17th Assembly minutes](../markdown/ga17_1989.md), section 17-106, statement of facts and judgment, printed pages 232 and 233.

## Appeal synopses

<a id="a01"></a>
### A01. 2007-16: Grady v. Southwest Florida Presbytery

Disposition: affirmed in part and reversed in part; censure and dissolution vacated; remanded.

A minister appealed his conviction and suspension after conflict over Presbytery's demand that he resign his pastorate. The SJC upheld the conviction but vacated the censure and dissolution of his call because Presbytery had treated refusal to follow its resignation directive as censurable disobedience. It remanded for reconsideration of censure under the correct constitutional understanding. The existing administrative suspension remained effective, so the decision did not immediately restore him to active ministry.

Benchmark lesson: distinguish conviction, censure, dissolution, and administrative suspension. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga37_2009__2007-16.md), judgments and reasoning on issue 6.

<a id="a02"></a>
### A02. 2002-01: Sang Bai Kim and Chan Soo Kim v. Korean Eastern Presbytery

Disposition: convictions upheld; increased censures held erroneous.

Two ruling elders appealed after Presbytery upheld disciplinary convictions and increased the Session's censures to deposition and excommunication. The SJC upheld the underlying judgments but held that an appellate court could not impose a greater censure than the trial court had imposed. New charges arising during an appeal likewise belonged first before the court of original jurisdiction. The successful challenge concerned the increased punishment, not exoneration from the original offenses.

Benchmark lesson: a successful appeal can leave guilt intact. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga31_2003__2002-01.md), judgment and reasoning specific to the case.

<a id="a03"></a>
### A03. 2012-07: Mitchell v. Presbytery of the Ascension

Disposition: one judgment vacated and remanded; another reversed and its specification dismissed.

A ruling elder appealed discipline for allegedly breaking a promise to take a sabbatical and failing in his duties as Session clerk. The SJC vacated the promise-breaking judgment because a crucial email was absent from the record, remanding for Presbytery to consider a new trial with that evidence. It reversed and dismissed the clerk-duty specification as insufficient to warrant the judicial proceeding. The two specifications therefore ended differently: one could be retried, while the other was dismissed.

Benchmark lesson: “partially sustained” alone obscures two successful challenges with different remedies. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga42_2014__2012-07.md), judgments and reasoning on specifications 2 and 6.

<a id="a04"></a>
### A04. 2018-01: Mapes v. Metropolitan New York Presbytery

Disposition: judgment affirmed in part and reversed in part; censures replaced with admonition.

A ruling elder appealed discipline arising from his conduct at a closed meeting of a temporary Session. The SJC upheld the finding that he failed to submit to church government, but reversed the remaining judgment and found suspension from the sacraments and deposition excessive. It substituted admonition and restored him to the sacraments and his office. The decision did not validate every aspect of the temporary Session's formation or trial, which included matters not raised in his specifications of error.

Benchmark lesson: reduced censure is not acquittal, and assumed validity for deciding an appeal is not a holding of constitutional validity. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga48_2021__2018-01.md), case summary, judgment, and reasoning. The moot complaint is companion docket 2018-04.

<a id="a05"></a>
### A05. 2021-13: Dudt v. Northwest Georgia Presbytery

Disposition: appeal sustained; judgments reversed in whole; not guilty; restored to office.

A ruling elder was convicted after emailing an SJC decision to the congregation and urging delay of a congregational meeting. The SJC reversed the judgments in whole, found him not guilty, and restored him to office. The Session could decline to distribute the decision itself but could not prohibit his distribution merely by invoking submission to his brethren. The record also failed to establish the charged false statements. His acquittal did not depend on every alleged procedural error succeeding.

Benchmark lesson: identify both the constitutional limit and the evidentiary failure; do not reduce acquittal to “complaint sustained.” Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga49_2022__2021-13.md), judgment and explanatory minutes on the email charges.

<a id="a06"></a>
### A06. 2005-01: Chastain v. Heritage Presbytery

Disposition: conviction and censure upheld despite procedural errors.

A minister appealed his conviction for divisive conduct, arguing that his letters disputing Session decisions did not constitute a scriptural offense and that the censure and process were defective. The SJC upheld the conviction and censure. It distinguished the permissibility of his underlying views from the divisive manner of advancing them to the congregation after Session decisions. Although it found procedural errors, it held that none required reversal or remand.

Benchmark lesson: “errors found” does not mean the appeal succeeded. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga34_2006__2005-01.md), issues, judgment, and majority reasoning. Do not substitute the dissent's view of BCO 29-1 for the holding.

<a id="a07"></a>
### A07. 1999-05: Rountree v. Covenant Presbytery

Disposition: challenge denied; divestiture without censure upheld.

A minister challenged divestiture without censure after remaining on Presbytery's roll for more than seven years without a call while living outside its bounds. The SJC upheld the divestiture under BCO 13-2 and 34-10. Attempts to arrange a transfer had failed, no particular ministry was in prospect, and his residence outside Presbytery's bounds impeded its oversight. Presbytery had followed the required two-meeting process and acted within its constitutional responsibilities.

Benchmark lesson: identify whose position was sustained, especially in older disposition language. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga29_2001__1999-05.md), issue, judgment, and reasoning.

<a id="a08"></a>
### A08. 2019-05: Goggan v. Missouri Presbytery

Disposition: specifications of error not sustained. Brief adopted reasoning.

A church member appealed disciplinary convictions following a dispute involving accusations against his pastor and Session. The SJC rejected the specifications of error because the appellant had not demonstrated sufficient reason to sustain them. The SJC did not approve the panel's proposed decision; its final decision supplied only this brief explanation, leaving no adopted issue-by-issue analysis of the alleged errors.

Benchmark lesson: distinguish the final adopted explanation from a panel proposal. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga48_2021__2019-05.md), facts, judgment, and final reasoning.

<a id="a09"></a>
### A09. 1999-04: Fitzsimmons v. Evangel Presbytery

Disposition: administratively out of order. Short-record example.

The SJC found the appeal administratively out of order because it was not filed with the Stated Clerk of the General Assembly within the required thirty-day period. The ruling enforced the filing deadline and did not decide the underlying case on its merits.

Benchmark lesson: name the missing filing and deadline, not just “procedural defect.” Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga28_2000__1999-04.md).

<a id="a10"></a>
### A10. 2004-09: Robar v. Central Carolina Presbytery

Disposition: judicially out of order.

The SJC found a ruling elder's appeal judicially out of order for improper filing under BCO 42-4. The dissent explained that he had filed with Presbytery's clerk but not the Assembly's clerk after receiving incorrect advice from Presbytery, and argued that the mistake should be excused. That equitable argument did not prevail. The adopted ruling gives the filing defect, not a substantive review of his conviction, as the basis for rejecting the appeal.

Benchmark lesson: useful factual context supplied in a dissent must be identified as such. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga33_2005__2004-09.md), adopted ruling and Duncan dissent.

<a id="a11"></a>
### A11. 2023-19: Huffman v. Tennessee Valley Presbytery

Disposition: judicially out of order as an appeal; remanded for adjudication as a complaint.

A minister sought review of discipline in a case without process that was nearly complete when BCO 38-1 changed the available remedy from complaint to appeal. The SJC held that retroactive application would be unfair. It found the appeal judicially out of order but remanded for Presbytery to hear the matter as a complaint under the former rule, resetting the procedural clock. The ruling preserved a route to review without deciding the discipline's merits.

Benchmark lesson: the historical rule change is essential context, not expendable chronology. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga51_2024__2023-19.md), ruling on appeal, before the unrelated manual proposals.

<a id="a12"></a>
### A12. 1986-04: Steele v. Great Lakes Presbytery

Disposition: abandoned.

A minister appealed his divestiture without censure, challenging the absence of judicial process and Presbytery's treatment of his ministry and membership. The Assembly deemed the appeal abandoned because no one appeared to represent it. The adjudication did not decide whether the divestiture was constitutionally proper or resolve the appellant's allegations of unequal treatment.

Benchmark lesson: distinguish the appellant's stated objections from an adjudicated finding. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga14_1986__case4.md), appeal and adopted adjudication.

<a id="a13"></a>
### A13. 2002-06: Wright v. Northern California Presbytery

Disposition: out of order and withdrawn. Short-record example.

The SJC report records the appeal as found out of order and withdrawn. It does not specify whether the out-of-order finding was administrative or judicial, explain the defect, or decide the merits.

Benchmark lesson: retain both reported events without adding an unsupported sequence or subtype. Evidence: [case page](../cases/ga31_2003__2002-06.md) and its linked Assembly report.

<a id="a14"></a>
### A14. 2004-11: Scott v. Northern California Presbytery

Disposition: moot. Short-record example.

The Assembly report records the appeal as moot. The published entry does not identify the event that made it moot or provide a merits ruling.

Benchmark lesson: a missing reason is a source limit, not an invitation to infer settlement or withdrawal. Evidence: [docket excerpt](../cases/ga33_2005__stub_2004-11.md).

<a id="a15"></a>
### A15. 2007-05: Mitchell v. Evangel Presbytery

Disposition: withdrawn. Short-record example.

The SJC report records the appeal as withdrawn. It provides no reason for the withdrawal and no ruling on the underlying dispute.

Benchmark lesson: no merits holding can be extracted from a withdrawal notice. Evidence: [docket excerpt](../cases/ga35_2007__stub_2007-05.md).

<a id="a16"></a>
### A16. 2008-12: Malone v. Metropolitan New York Presbytery

Disposition: out of order. Short-record example.

The SJC report lists the appeal among cases found out of order. The entry does not identify the procedural defect or distinguish an administrative finding from a judicial one. It supplies no merits decision.

Benchmark lesson: use the source's level of specificity even when the taxonomy permits finer labels. Evidence: [docket excerpt](../cases/ga37_2009__stub_2008-12.md).

<a id="a17"></a>
### A17. 1992-10: Bernard v. Presbytery of the Ascension

Disposition: administratively out of order; appeal must first be heard by Presbytery.

Members appealed excommunication directly to the General Assembly after a presbytery-appointed commission acting as their Session imposed the censure. The SJC held the appeal administratively out of order because the next appellate court was Presbytery. A commission's appointment by Presbytery did not mean that its sessional judgment had already received presbytery appellate review. The appeal had to proceed through that court before reaching the Assembly.

Benchmark lesson: institutional origin and the capacity in which a body acts are different jurisdictional questions. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga21_1993__1992-10.md), disposition.

<a id="a18"></a>
### A18. 2010-03: McNeil v. Chesapeake Presbytery

Disposition: judicially out of order.

A member who refused to plead or appear before a presbytery judicial commission appealed the resulting suspension from the sacraments. The SJC held that he had not submitted to a regular trial and therefore was not entitled to appeal under BCO 42-2. Treating the filing as a complaint would not cure it: he had not first complained to Presbytery, and the filing period had expired. The ruling separately preserved the distinction between challenging the censure and complaining against later court actions.

Benchmark lesson: do not suggest that censure itself extinguishes every possible challenge to that censure. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga39_2011__2010-03.md), rulings 2 to 4 and rationale.

<a id="a19"></a>
### A19. 2011-13: Spann v. Oak Mountain Presbyterian Church

Disposition: appeal dismissed as premature, subject to evidence of the necessary prior proceedings.

A member alleged that her church had effectively excommunicated her without proper process and sought review directly from the SJC. The record showed neither a judicial decision by the church nor an appeal to Presbytery. The SJC dismissed the appeal as premature unless she could supply evidence of those proceedings, and requested a written Session response through Presbytery. The ruling did not find that the alleged excommunication or abuse had occurred.

Benchmark lesson: preserve both the conditional dismissal and the request for a response. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga40_2012__2011-13.md), adopted decision before subsequent material.

<a id="a20"></a>
### A20. 2013-12: Marshall v. Pacific Presbytery

Disposition: appeal dismissed; lower judgment suspended indefinitely; Presbytery rebuked.

Presbytery failed to transmit a material part of the record needed for an appeal, causing injury to the appellant that could not be remedied. The SJC suspended the lower judgment indefinitely and dismissed the case under BCO 42-7. It also rebuked Presbytery and urged greater care in preserving, transcribing, and transmitting testimony. Dismissal thus did not leave the challenged judgment enforceable or signify an unsuccessful challenge on the merits.

Benchmark lesson: the effect of dismissal can be favorable to the appellant; the disposition label alone is inadequate. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga43_2015__2013-12.md), adopted decision, not the concurrence's practical suggestions.

## Judicial-reference synopses

<a id="j01"></a>
### J01. 2017-06: Reference from Blue Ridge Presbytery

Disposition: reference denied; Presbytery directed to hear the complaint.

Presbytery asked the SJC to hear and decide a complaint rather than adjudicate it locally. The SJC denied the reference because the record did not demonstrate bias requiring all presbyters' disqualification, serious division, constitutional questions, or difficult issues warranting that course. Presbytery was directed to hear the complaint itself. The companion complaint was answered by this decision; neither ruling determined the underlying complaint's merits.

Benchmark lesson: refusal of a reference is not denial of the underlying complaint. Evidence: [combined decision](../cases/ga46_2018__2017-06_2017-07.md). Docket 2017-07 is the companion complaint, not a second reference example.

<a id="j02"></a>
### J02. 2002-04: Judicial reference from Evangel Presbytery

Disposition: request not acceded to. Short-record example.

Evangel Presbytery requested that the SJC accept a judicial reference for adjudication. The SJC did not accede to the request. The published report gives no reason for declining it and no judgment on the underlying matter.

Benchmark lesson: report refusal to accept the proceeding without implying that its substantive claims failed. Evidence: [docket excerpt](../cases/ga31_2003__stub_2002-04.md).

<a id="j03"></a>
### J03. 1992-08: Reference from Central Carolina Presbytery

Disposition: reference declined; matters returned to Presbytery.

Presbytery referred several interconnected complaints and disciplinary matters involving a congregation to the SJC. Other related complaints had not been referred, and the intended scope of the SJC's authority was unclear. The SJC declined adjudication and returned the matters to Presbytery because deciding the referred cases could make the others moot without due consideration. The decision avoided that consequence; it did not declare the reference itself moot.

Benchmark lesson: a possible consequence discussed in reasoning is not an actual disposition. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga21_1993__1992-08.md), disposition and explanation of the reference's scope.

<a id="j04"></a>
### J04. 2022-10: PCA v. Daniel Herron

Disposition: not guilty on all charges; censures and administrative suspensions removed.

On a presbytery reference, the SJC tried a minister on charges arising from allegations of sexual harassment, bullying, and related conduct, including a civil defamation suit against accusers. It acquitted him on all charges and restored him to good standing. Acting as the finder of fact, it found the prosecution had not met its burden. It also rejected a categorical prohibition on Christians suing other Christians: the prosecution had to establish why this particular suit was sinful.

Benchmark lesson: this is an original trial through BCO 41, not appellate deference or a BCO 34-1 petition. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga50_2023__2022-10.md), summary, verdicts, reasoning A to D, and conclusion.

<a id="j05"></a>
### J05. 1985-02: Judicial reference from Eastern Canada Presbytery

Disposition: reference not received.

Presbytery referred charges against two ruling elders to the General Assembly. The Assembly declined to receive the reference because the charged parties had renounced PCA jurisdiction. It directed Presbytery to handle the matter under BCO 38-4 rather than adjudicating the charges through the requested reference.

Benchmark lesson: refusal for lack of jurisdiction is not an acquittal. Evidence: [13th Assembly minutes](../markdown/ga13_1985.md), adopted recommendation for Case 2, item 13-43.

<a id="j06"></a>
### J06. 1998-10: Curtis v. Eastern Carolina Presbytery, reference

Disposition: returned to Presbytery to hear the complaint.

Presbytery prohibited a minister from teaching his confessional exceptions, then referred his complaint and a related complaint to the Assembly without first hearing his objection. The SJC returned the matter for Presbytery to adjudicate. Although Presbytery described its request as seeking nonjudicial advice, the matters were judicial complaints already in process. Uncertainty about the intended reference, together with the lower court's responsibility to hear the case, favored returning it without deciding the teaching restriction.

Benchmark lesson: retain the distinction between advice and adjudication and the merits question left open. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga28_2000__1998-10.md), recommendation and reasoning.

## Original-jurisdiction-request synopses

<a id="o01"></a>
### O01. 2022-12: BCO 34-1 petitions concerning Missouri Presbytery

Disposition: assumption of jurisdiction unavailable after withdrawal.

Two presbyteries asked the Assembly to assume original jurisdiction over alleged doctrinal errors of a minister. Before the SJC decided the petitions, the minister and his congregation left the PCA and his name was removed from Presbytery's roll. The SJC concluded that it could not assume original jurisdiction because the PCA no longer had jurisdiction over him. The decision did not adjudicate the alleged doctrinal errors.

Benchmark lesson: jurisdictional disposition is not doctrinal vindication. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga50_2023__2022-12.md), decision on petitions, before the attached presbytery letter.

<a id="o02"></a>
### O02. 1999-01: Western Carolina and other presbyteries' original-jurisdiction request

Disposition: administratively out of order; not properly before the Assembly.

Three presbyteries sought original jurisdiction over a minister concerning a Session's decision to permit a woman to speak from the pulpit. The SJC held the requests administratively out of order. Responsibility for the conduct of local worship belonged to the Session, and the minister's presbytery had acted on the allegations rather than refused to act. The ruling rejected this use of BCO 34-1; it did not decide that the disputed worship practice was permissible.

Benchmark lesson: identify both the proper responsible body and the missing refusal-to-act condition. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga28_2000__1999-01.md), judgment and majority reasoning, not the attached presbytery resolutions.

<a id="o03"></a>
### O03. 2006-03: Calvary Presbytery request concerning S. Wilkins

Disposition: out of order. Short-record example.

Calvary Presbytery requested original jurisdiction in the Wilkins matter. The SJC report lists the request as out of order, without specifying the defect or rendering a substantive judgment on the allegations.

Benchmark lesson: neither a missing second petitioner nor another jurisdictional defect should be guessed from this notice. Evidence: [docket excerpt](../cases/ga34_2006__stub_2006-03.md).

<a id="o04"></a>
### O04. 2020-02: Requests to assume original jurisdiction concerning Missouri Presbytery

Disposition: answered by reference to decisions in 2020-12 and 2020-05.

The SJC answered three presbyteries' BCO 34-1 requests by reference to its decisions in two Speck complaints concerning Missouri Presbytery: the October 21, 2021 decision in 2020-12 and the March 3, 2022 decision in 2020-05. The brief disposition supplied no independent reasoning or order transferring the requests to another court. Their answer lies in those incorporated decisions.

<a id="o05"></a>
### O05. 2022-11: BCO 34-1 petitions concerning Central Indiana Presbytery

Disposition: answered and decided by reference to the Herron trial decision.

The SJC postponed consideration of the original-jurisdiction petitions until completion of the Herron trial. After rendering that trial decision, it answered and decided the petitions by reference to it. The petitions therefore received a disposition tied to the completed trial, not an order transferring them elsewhere. The trial itself had proceeded through a BCO 41 reference, a distinct procedural vehicle.

## BCO 40-5 synopses

<a id="b01"></a>
### B01. 2006-02: Central Carolina Presbytery memorial concerning Louisiana Presbytery

Disposition: procedural compliance found; substantive failure found; citation and ensuing trial directed.

A memorial challenged Presbytery's handling of a minister's theological views. After requiring further examination, the SJC found that Presbytery had complied with its investigative directions but had not reached a constitutionally proper substantive conclusion. It cited Presbytery under BCO 40-5 and directed preparation of an indictment in the ensuing case. The proceeding concerned Presbytery's discharge of its duties, not an SJC trial of the minister himself or a BCO 34-1 assumption of jurisdiction over him.

Benchmark lesson: procedural compliance does not settle the adequacy of the resulting judgment. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga36_2008__2006-02.md), Part II judgment and reasoning; ensuing trial 2007-14 is a separate docket.

<a id="b02"></a>
### B02. 2019-06: PCA v. Presbytery of the Mississippi Valley

Disposition: errors found in Presbytery's response to a BCO 40-5 report.

Presbytery approved a Session's treatment of a member who pursued divorce despite its counsel, pleaded not guilty to disobedience, and was then erased from the roll. The SJC found constitutional errors in Presbytery's response to her report. Respectful disagreement with counsel was not itself disobedience, and a not-guilty plea did not establish abandonment of membership vows. The Session also failed to address membership in another branch of the church and to notify her of erasure.

Benchmark lesson: distinguish adjudicating an underlying alleged sin from treating disagreement with advice as the sin. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga48_2021__2019-06.md), judgment and reasoning A to E. Do not turn this into a holding that the divorce was biblically justified.

<a id="b03"></a>
### B03. 2023-14: BCO 40-5 report concerning Northwest Georgia Presbytery

Disposition: egregious error found; annulment of installations refused; proceedings concluded.

Presbytery installed three associate pastors following a constitutionally deficient election. The SJC found egregious error, identifying slate voting, a standing rather than ballot vote, and failure to record the number of voters present under the rule then in force. It nevertheless refused the requested annulment of the installations, citing the absence of PCA precedent and doubts about that remedy's legitimacy. It concluded the proceedings. Finding constitutional error therefore did not mean granting the requested relief.

Benchmark lesson: distinguish the adverse finding from the refused remedy and preserve the historical voting rule. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga51_2024__2023-14.md), operative ruling and reasons. “Report denied” would be misleading.

<a id="b04"></a>
### B04. 2007-14: PCA v. Louisiana Presbytery

Disposition: one specification dismissed after trial; guilty plea on another; admonition imposed.

In proceedings over Presbytery's handling of a minister's theological views, the SJC dismissed one specification because the alleged investigative steps had not been shown to be constitutionally required. Presbytery pleaded guilty to the separate failure to find a strong presumption of guilt and proceed to trial, and the SJC imposed admonition. The dismissal did not vindicate Presbytery's handling of the matter as a whole, and the proceeding did not determine the minister's guilt at an SJC trial.

Benchmark lesson: preserve specification-level verdicts and identify who was tried. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga36_2008__2007-14.md), verdict, reasoning, and imposition of censure.

<a id="b05"></a>
### B05. 2015-05: Thompson application concerning Granada Presbyterian Church

An applicant asked the SJC to cite a Session for alleged unconstitutional use of discipline before Presbytery had considered his application. The SJC dismissed the filing as administratively out of order. The ruling did not decide the alleged misconduct.

Benchmark lesson: name the proper initial court; “no final disposition” conceals an actual dismissal. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga44_2016__2015-05.md), adopted decision.

Benchmark lesson: repeated parties do not establish identical proceedings or outcomes. Evidence: [docket excerpt](../cases/ga46_2018__stub_2017-08.md).

## Review-and-control synopses

<a id="r01"></a>
### R01. 2017-10: In re Korean Eastern Presbytery

Disposition: citation complied with; materials referred for records review.

The SJC found that Presbytery had complied with its citation by providing the materials required under BCO 40-1. It referred those materials to the Committee for Review of Presbytery Records. Compliance with the production requirement did not itself constitute substantive approval of everything in the submitted records.

Benchmark lesson: identify the duty satisfied and the review still to occur. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga46_2018__2017-10.md).

<a id="r02"></a>
### R02. 2017-11: In re Korean Southwest Presbytery

Disposition: adopted responses accepted as citation compliance; forwarded for records review.

The SJC accepted Presbytery's formally approved responses as complying with its citation and noted that they had been forwarded to the Review of Presbytery Records Committee. The decision concerns compliance and routing of responses; it does not set out a separate merits ruling on the original exceptions.

Benchmark lesson: do not mistake acceptance for filing purposes for adjudication of every exception. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga46_2018__2017-11.md).

<a id="r03"></a>
### R03. 2021-08: In re Korean Southwest Orange County Presbytery

Disposition: response process ongoing at the March 3, 2022 reporting stage.

Presbytery had not supplied formally adopted responses to records-review exceptions by the SJC's March meeting, although its clerk had prepared drafts. The SJC allowed time for Presbytery to adopt and file responses, with prompt forwarding to the records-review committee if received. Otherwise, it would require an appearance at a called hearing. The report records an unfinished compliance process, not a determination that the proposed responses were satisfactory.

Benchmark lesson: distinguish a clerk's draft from court-adopted action, and date an unresolved status. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga49_2022__2021-08.md), March 3, 2022 section only; adjacent complaint material is not part of this case.

<a id="r04"></a>
### R04. 2015-06: PCA v. South Florida Presbytery

Disposition: most responses satisfactory; one unsatisfactory; further records obligations identified.

Responding to an Assembly citation, Presbytery submitted adopted answers to outstanding records-review exceptions. The SJC accepted most answers but rejected one that did not address the required handling of charges against a minister. It also identified a further exception concerning divestiture and directed attention to unfinished Session-record reviews. The outcome was a set of specific supervisory findings and continuing obligations, not an absence of any disposition.

Benchmark lesson: supervisory review needs a descriptive outcome even when no party “wins.” Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga44_2016__2015-06.md), adopted actions and grounds.

<a id="r05"></a>
### R05. 2016-10: In re Korean Northwest Presbytery

Disposition: amended and corrected responses accepted. Short-record example.

Presbytery reported that it had amended and formally adopted its responses to records-review exceptions. The SJC accepted the amended and corrected responses. The brief decision records that supervisory action but does not publish an analysis of the individual exceptions.

Benchmark lesson: an affirmative administrative or supervisory decision is not “no final disposition” merely because it is not a merits opinion in an adversarial case. Evidence: [case text](../cases/ga46_2018__2016-10.md).
