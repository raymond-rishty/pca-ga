## Minutes transcript

B.** Considered a constitutional inquiry from Philadelphia Presbytery: "At its May 11, 2002, stated meeting, Philadelphia Presbytery voted to make a constitutional inquiry to the Constitutional Business Committee of the General Assembly, to wit:

Having accepted an appeal from a church member against a judgment rendered against him by the session of his church, and having referred that appeal to a duly appointed commission properly erected by Presbytery, does the Presbytery have the right:

1. to take back from said commission the matter committed to it before the commission can complete its task and render a judgment; and

2. to reverse, without hearing the appeal, the decision of the Session, which found the appellant guilty and suspended him from the office of ruling elder?

The actual case in dispute has been in the commission's hands for sixteen months and the record of the case has still not been able to be finalized to the satisfaction of the appellant, the session, and the commission.

Philadelphia Presbytery needs and requests the advice of our fathers and brothers."

It is the opinion of the CCBthat in reference to question #1 of the inquiry, our answer is yes, the Presbytery may take back from said commission the matter committed to it before the commission has completed its task and rendered a judgment.

In reference to question #2, our answer is no, the Presbytery may not reverse, without hearing the appeal, the decision of the Session. The Presbytery must follow the procedures detailed in BCO 42.
