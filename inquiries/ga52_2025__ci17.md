Generally speaking, evaluating repentance is not as simple as the BCO might seem to imply. BCO 30-1 uses the phrase "remains impenitent" but doesn't suggest a definition. I've read that Spurgeon once said: "Remember that the man who truly repents is never satisfied with his own repentance. We can no more repent perfectly than we can live perfectly. However pure our tears, there will always be some dirt in them; there will always be something to be repented of even in our best repentance." Most of us have undisclosed sins of thought which, if they were put in a signed document and distributed to our peers, would probably result in considerable loss respect for us. Is our repentance "real" if we don't confess them openly? Why don't we lay ourselves at the mercy of our courts for our private sins? Put another way, why would a man's confession in a CWOP only be considered "real" if he doesn't care what censure is imposed, but we regard our repentance as real despite being content to keep our private thoughts just between ourselves and God?
