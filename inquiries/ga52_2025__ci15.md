## Verbatim record

It's my understanding that in US criminal courts an accused person sometimes offers to plead or is invited by the prosecutor to plead guilty to a lesser charge rather than going to trial on a greater charge. But in the PCA, we don't have statutory minimum and maximum sentences specified for offenses. Nor do we have defined 1st, 2nd, and 3rd degree offenses. Nevertheless, nothing precludes a Session or Presbytery (or one of its committees or commissions) from informing a prospective confessor what BCO censure it will recommend or impose. And a confessor is free to decline to co-sign the BCO 38-1 document unless he is apprised of the recommended or intended censure. Granted, that could result in a trial instead, but it could also result in a satisfactory resolution of the matter outside of a trial.

Ordinarily, if I were advising a minister considering a CWOP, and the proposed document didn't mention what censure would be recommended or imposed, I might encourage him to consider not signing unless he was willing to accept the severest censure. And if the confessor was not willing to sign without knowing the recommended or intended censure, the committee or commission would need to discuss whether to (1) proceed to formal judicial process (a cumbersome and often divisive path), or (2) collaborate with the confessor or his counsel to get to a point at which he could sign. That might not sound very ecclesiastical, but it's sensible.
