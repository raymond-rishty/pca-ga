# IV.B — Whether Minutes of Congregational Meetings must be submitted as part of Session records reviewed by Presbytery

*The CCB ruled that congregational meeting minutes are part of session records subject to presbytery review, and sessions must submit them if the presbytery requires it.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Constitutional inquiry  ·  **Assembly:** 52nd (2025)  ·  **Provisions:** BCO 41-3, BCO 40-1, BCO 40, BCO 25-5  ·  **Disposition:** CCB acceded to the reference; Congregational meeting minutes are part of Session records subject to Presbytery review; if Presbytery requires them, Sessions must submit them; Adopted 8-0-0

*Source: [ga52_2025 lines 15036–15063](../markdown/ga52_2025.md#ga52-p260)*

## Verbatim record

Regarding the assertion about conscience made in TE Svendsen's Complaint, sustained by Presbytery in May 2023, we are not persuaded the VPC Session preference for benedictions over doxologies would reasonably result in a violation of a minister's conscience. In his Complaint sustained at the May 2023 meeting, TE Svendsen contended that when Presbytery allowed the VPC Session decision to stand, the Presbytery had "bound the conscience of a minister by requiring him to follow his session's motion." He contended, "If it is not scripturally required, then it is a commandment of men over a matter of worship and cannot be anything less that the binding of the conscience." Similar arguments were made by Presbytery in its Brief in this present Case.[^fn-ga52-p892-n18]8

However, we are not persuaded the Session's July 2022 decision involved a matter like what is referenced in Westminster Confession of Faith 20.2:

> "God alone is Lord of the conscience, and hath left it free from the doctrines and commandments of men, which are, in anything, contrary to his Word; or beside it, if matters of faith, or worship. So that, to believe such doctrines, or to obey such commands, out of conscience, is to betray true liberty of conscience: and the requiring of an implicit faith, and an absolute and blind obedience, is to destroy liberty of conscience, and reason also." [^fn-ga52-p892-n19]

While the minister believed he should have more freedom in how he closed worship services, it is not reasonable to see how the Session's restriction would have caused the minister to sin. And if the minister's conscience deemed it as sin, he was free to ask (and should ask) an eligible elder to issue the benediction instead.

Finally, an ongoing liturgical dispute between a long-time pastor and a majority of elders on a Session might be something reasonably expected to harm the peace of the Church, or something unsuited to promote the welfare of the Church. It might also be reasonable to expect this given there have been three complaints to Presbytery from the Pastor and members of the VPC Session on this matter, and now this present one to the SJC. One cannot read the Record of this Case without wondering how the ordination promise of "subjection to your brethren in the Lord" was applied. (BCO 21-5, vow 4 and BCO 24-6, vow 5) However, the prior Martinez/Cook Complaint contended this matter had not disrupted the collegiality of the Session, and that contention was not disputed in the adjudication of that Complaint or in this present one. Regardless, the effect of this disagreement on the Session, or on the Pastor, or on Valley Presbyterian Church was not an issue raised in the Svendsen Complaint before Presbytery in October 2023, or in this present one.

The Panel's proposed decision was initially drafted by RE Donahoe, and after amendments, the Panel adopted it by vote of 3-0. The SJC reviewed each part of the proposed decision and approved the final version of the Decision by vote of 19-1, with 4 absent.
