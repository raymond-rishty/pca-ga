# 29-28 Item 4 — Rendering Opinion on Assembly Action

*The CCB advised that the SJC may only address matters assigned to it, but once assigned a matter may render an opinion on the legitimacy of the GA action that prompted the referral.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Constitutional inquiry  ·  **Assembly:** 29th (2001)  ·  **Disposition:** advice given

*Source: [ga29_2001 lines 6237–6259](../markdown/ga29_2001.md#ga29-p145)*

---

## Digest headnote
*Editorial summary from the PCA Digest, Part II (Interpretations of the Constitution) — this is the Digest's wording, not the verbatim minutes. The authoritative text is the verbatim record below / linked above.*

The CCB answered a constitutional inquiry arising from SJC Case 99-1 asking whether the SJC has the authority to render an opinion on the legitimacy of an action of the General Assembly if not specifically asked to do so. The CCB advised the Assembly that the SJC may only render an opinion on the matters assigned to it. Once it is assigned a matter, the SJC may render an opinion even as to the legitimacy of an action in which the GA refers business to the SJC.

COMMUNICATION #4 From Western Carolina Presbytery (to B&O) "A call for Purity, Peace and Unity of the Church"

Fathers and Brethren: Two years ago, Western Carolina Presbytery initiated inquiry into the question of a woman preaching at the Cedar Springs Presbyterian Church of Tennessee Valley Presbytery, and also into the views of the Rev. John Wood on the subject of women preaching. Our concern, shared by many throughout the denomination, was that a woman should not be allowed to preach in a PCA pulpit. Tolerating false doctrine and false practice is one way in which other denominations have degenerated in the past. We believe that failing to express our concern would not have fostered the purity, peace and unity of our Church. It has been our wholehearted desire to encourage and promote the peace of the church. While we confess that we have been far from perfect in that quest, we believe that we can, with clear conscience, testify to the fact that we were earnest and sincere in all of our actions with regard to the John Wood matter. It was never our intention or desire that the Cedar Springs Presbyterian Church should leave the PCA. We wanted to know the facts. Our hope all along was for this congregation and its pastor to conform to the obvious teaching of Holy Scripture with regard to female preaching. With the events that have transpired since that time, and the departure of the Cedar Springs Presbyterian Church from the PCA, Western Carolina Presbytery desires to issue a call to the whole PCA to bring closure to this matter and to endeavor to pursue together both the purity, peace and unity of the Church. A Committee of Western Carolina Presbytery interviewed members of the Standing Judicial Commission regarding various reports that had been circulated and found that the investigative committee of the SJC found and reported that there were no discrepancies in the testimony of witnesses. Mr. Wood acknowledged that the testimony of the witnesses was accurate, and he stated before the panel "he should have been more careful to articulate his belief that women should not be ordained to the teaching or ruling eldership and that they should not be permitted to 'preach' in the traditional sense of authoritative teaching from the word of God, and teaching elders are called and ordained to do." Though one may differ with the finding of the SJC, the WCP Committee found that the SJC had a full and frank deliberation of the investigating panel's report and voted unanimously that "the investigation has not resulted in a strong resumption of guilt on the part of TE John Wood... and therefore judicial process should not be instituted against TE John Wood for his expressed views regarding women." We accept the decision of the SJC. The WCP investigation found that the SJC followed the Manual of Operations in determining to disqualify any one of the SJC members from participating in the deliberation.

It is because of all the controversy and alleged irregularities surrounding the John Wood matter that we, once again, found ourselves duty-bound to raise the question of possible impropriety when reports were circulated that one or more members of the SJC had deliberately sought to protect John Wood at the Vision 2000 Caucus, but had not recused themselves from debating and voting. The WCP Committee investigated these allegations, and are happy to report that we have not been able to confirm them. In a statement made to two members of our Presbytery, RE John White, Chairman of the SJC, stated that he had not tried to advocate any particular course of action for the Assembly to take prior to the meeting of the Assembly itself. Mr. White indicated that at a meeting of the Vision 2000 Caucus several months prior to the 2000 General Assembly, he had set forth what he thought was the Constitutional position, viz., that the Assembly could not overturn the SJC's determination in the John Wood matter. We hereby report Mr. White's recounting of the facts, in the hope that the rumor that one or more SJC members have engaged in improper lobbying regarding the John Wood matter can now be put to rest. As we come to closure on the John Wood Judicial Matter, we hope and pray that all of us in the Presbyterian Church in America have learned from it. Perhaps the foremost lesson is that in order to maintain the bond peace (Ephesians 4:3-6), which we all desire, there must be genuine unity; and that, in order to have proper unity, humility (Eph. 4:2, Philippians 2:1-22) is the necessary foundation. Of course unity can only be maintained between differing brethren when there is a genuine respect for the integrity of all parties, a gentle patience that bears with one another (Eph. 4:2). For whatever the reason, there seems to have been a general breakdown of trust across the denomination. The biblical way to restore trust is for individuals to pursue each other to resolve differences and clear up misunderstanding and miscommunication (Matthew 5 and 18). We pray that the Lord may be pleased to restore peace to us and maintain purity, peace and unity among us. Again, we urge that the John Wood matter be put to rest by this year's General Assembly, recognizing that she must handle the several questions that have been raised by Ascension and Calvary Presbyteries. We trust that we can now move forward as a denomination, in the bonds of purity, peace and unity in which we can all rejoice.

Adopted at a called meeting of Western Carolina Presbytery on May 8, 2001. Attested by: /s/ Skip Gilliken, Stated Clerk
