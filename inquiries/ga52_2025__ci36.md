## Verbatim record

<a id="ga52-p904"></a>
<!-- PAGE ga=52 pdf_page=904 printed_page=902 -->

Citing an example of a doxology that ought not be used for the closing of public worship, the complainants note that Jude 24-25 is considered by most English translators of the Bible to be a doxology.[^fn-ga52-p904-n29] However, we believe that Jude 24-25 is very appropriate for the encouragement and blessing of God's people.

Jude's epistle was written to people who had been secretly invaded by false Christians causing trouble among the genuine believers. It is notable then that Jude closes his epistle by announcing that God “is able to keep [them] from stumbling and to present [them] blameless before the presence of his glory with great joy..." A greater blessing or word of encouragement than this can scarcely be imagined. Whether the direction of this word is primarily “God-ward” or "manward," these verses seem as though they would be a welcome affirmation of Christ's benefits in the ears of believers going out into a world that challenges their faith.
