# Supplemental II.1 — Whether the AC Committee of Commissioners may make a substitute motion nominating a Stated Clerk if the AC did not bring a nomination

*The CCB ruled that the Administrative Committee's Committee of Commissioners may not make a substitute nomination for Stated Clerk when the matter does not arise from AC recommendations.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Constitutional inquiry  ·  **Assembly:** 52nd (2025)  ·  **Provisions:** RAO 14-6.d, RAO 14-6.h, RAO 14-6.k  ·  **Disposition:** No — AC CoC may not make a substitute nomination for Stated Clerk if the matter does not arise in AC recommendations; Adopted 8-0-0

12-5. The church Session is charged with maintaining the spiritual government of the church, for which purpose it has power:

...e. To exercise, in accordance with the Directory for Worship, authority over the time and place of the preaching of the Word and the administration of the Sacraments, over all other religious services, over the music in the services, and over the uses to which the church building and associated properties may be put; to take the oversight of the singing in the public worship of God; to ensure that the Word of God is preached only by such men as are sufficiently qualified (BCO 4-4, 53-2, 1 Timothy 2:11-12); to assemble the people for worship when there is no minister; to determine the best measures for promoting the spiritual interests of the church and congregation; … (emphasis supplied).

Here the Constitution assigns the Session the responsibility to exercise authority over worship services. Such authority certainly includes decision making related to the order, sequence and inclusion of proper elements, consistent with the Directory for Worship.

In this case, the Session did not request performance of any action contrary to Scripture, the Constitution, or the Directory for Worship. The Session did not ask the Pastor to include any extra-Biblical element or activity in the worship service, and questions related to such elements were not presented or decided here. The Session did not attempt to direct the Pastor what or how to preach. The decision does not reach questions related to whether benedictions are "required" in a worship service. The outcome did not turn on, approve, or disapprove any party's proffered definition of the elements at issue. In the face of a narrow, but reasonable difference of opinion and emphasis, the question presented and decided was which party determines which Biblical elements appear in a worship service and in which order. The Commission's decision answers that question according to BCO 12-5(e).

DISSENTING OPINION Case 2023-20 RE John Martinez v. Pacific Presbytery TE David F. Coffin, Jr. June 10, 2024

I dissent from the Decision of the Standing Judicial Commission (SJC) in this case. I believe that the SJC has failed to rule according to the most basic principles of Biblical and Presbyterian polity.

First, consider the authorities I will assert in favor of my understanding of our wholesome tradition, included in an Appendix to this dissent. I trust that the reader will reflect upon these principles, as they are the foundation of all that I will argue in favor of the view I represent in this dissent. It is in the context of these authorities that I ask, what is the issue before us? It is claimed in the Commission's decision that the BCO recognizes the Session's authority in these matters, and that settles the question. This conclusion is founded upon BCO 12-5.e.
