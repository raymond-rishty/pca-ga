# 10-75 III, 8 — Ordination status and restoration after deposition

*A deposed officer has no ordination status. Restoration requires a proper call, reaffirmation of ordination vows, and laying on of hands; the court must proceed with caution under BCO 34-8 and 37-7.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Constitutional inquiry  ·  **Assembly:** 10th (1982)  ·  **Provisions:** BCO 30-5, BCO 34-8, BCO 37-7  ·  **Disposition:** advice given

*Source: [ga10_1982 minutes p. 99 / PDF p. 101](../markdown/ga10_1982.md#ga10-p101)*

## Digest headnote
*Editorial summary from the PCA Digest, Part II (Interpretations of the Constitution) — this is the Digest's wording, not the verbatim minutes. The authoritative text is the verbatim record below / linked above.*

That Reference 1 from the Presbytery of Central Florida concerning the ordination status of a deposed officer and the process of restoration be answered as follows: A person under the censure of deposition has no ordination status. In restoring a deposed minister, the court of jurisdiction should proceed with great caution in accordance with BCO 34-8 and 37-7; at minimum it must require reaffirmation of the ordination vows and laying on of hands, and the man must not be reordained until he has received a proper call.

## Verbatim record

### As referred / posed

Reference 1: From the Presbytery of Central Florida

(Designated a Reference at the direction of the Sub-Committee on Judicial Business)

The Presbytery of Central Florida requests that the following overture be presented to the Tenth General Assembly of the Presbyterian Church in America:

Whereas, BCO 36-7 declares that the censure of deposition is grounded in the judgment that an ordained person is “disqualified for the office of the Christian ministry (or Ruling Eldership, etc.);” and

Whereas, BCO 37-4, in providing for the restoration of a deposed officer allows the court to “declare you absolved from the said sentence of deposition” and to “restore you to said office;” and

Whereas, these provisions do not address the question of ordination status of the individual under consideration;

Now therefore, the Presbytery of Central Florida requests answers to the following questions:

1. While under the censure of deposition, what is the status of the ordination of the individual?

2. In the process of restoration to office, is it necessary for the adjudicating court to:

- **a.** Undertake the entire ordination process?

- **b.** Simply ask the constitutional questions for ordination?

- **c.** Simply declare the restored offender again fully qualified to execute the office from which he was deposed?

### CCB advice

8. That Reference 1, p. 49 be answered as follows: A person under the censure of deposition has no ordination status. In restoring a deposed minister, the court of jurisdiction should proceed with great caution (BCO 34-8, 37-7). At a minimum the court must require reaffirmation of the ordination vows and the laying on of hands. In no case shall the man be reordained until he has received a proper call. Adopted
