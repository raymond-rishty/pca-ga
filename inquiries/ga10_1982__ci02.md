# 10-75 III, 10 — Definition of 'voter' in congregational officer elections

*A voter under BCO 24-3 is a communing member in good standing present at the meeting; only those who cast a ballot count toward the majority required for election.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Constitutional inquiry  ·  **Assembly:** 10th (1982)  ·  **Provisions:** BCO 24-3  ·  **Disposition:** advice given

*Source: [ga10_1982 lines 4231–4231](../markdown/ga10_1982.md#ga10-p103)*

## Digest headnote
*Editorial summary from the PCA Digest, Part II (Interpretations of the Constitution) — this is the Digest's wording, not the verbatim minutes. The authoritative text is the verbatim record below / linked above.*

That Reference 3 which reads as follows: The Session of Vineville Presbyterian Church at its stated November 1981 meeting requested that the General Assembly's Judicial Committee provide an interpretation of 24-3 and 24-4 of the Book of Church Order with respect to what constitutes a voter (one who casts a vote for one or more candidates or one who casts a blank ballot). Be answered with the following advice: A person eligible to vote is defined in BCO 24-3 as a communing member in good and regular standing who is present at the congregational meeting called to elect officers. A majority vote of the voters present is required to elect. GROUNDS: This recommendation is a ratification of the answer already given by the Permanent Sub-Committee on Judicial Business.
