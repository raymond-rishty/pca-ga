# 27-43 III — Appeals in Judicial Cases

*An overture's final sentence conflicted with the Constitution because appeals lie only in judicial cases; the CCB advised substituting 'complaint' for 'appeal' to cure the conflict.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Overture/amendment advice  ·  **Assembly:** 27th (1999)  ·  **Provisions:** BCO 38-3  ·  **Disposition:** in conflict

*Source: [ga27_1999 lines 6769–6777](../markdown/ga27_1999.md#ga27-p146)*

---

## Digest headnote
*Editorial summary from the PCA Digest, Part II (Interpretations of the Constitution) — this is the Digest's wording, not the verbatim minutes. The authoritative text is the verbatim record below / linked above.*

In the opinion of the CCB, Overture 11 from Pittsburgh Presbytery ("Clarify Cases without Process") was in conflict with the Constitution in the last sentence of the recommended revision to BCO 38-3, in that an appeal can only be made in judicial cases. The CCB advised that the conflict would be eliminated if the word "complaint" were substituted for the word "appeal."

**Key words:** BCO 38-3

**Inquiry from:** Pittsburgh Presbytery

**In the minutes:** [ga27_1999 p.146](../markdown/ga27_1999.md#ga27-p146)
