# 16-69 V, 6 — Dividing presbytery to hear ordination sermons

*BCO 21-4 does not permit presbyteries to divide into committees or parts to hear ordination sermons. A presbytery may use a commission in exceptional circumstances, but the Assembly cautioned against making that a regular practice.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Constitutional inquiry  ·  **Assembly:** 16th (1988)  ·  **Provisions:** BCO 21-4  ·  **Disposition:** advice given

*Source: [ga16_1988 pp. 164–165](../markdown/ga16_1988.md#ga16-p166)*

---

## Digest headnote

The Assembly answered a constitutional inquiry asking whether BCO 21-4 permits a presbytery to divide into committees or parts to hear ordination candidates' sermons. It rejected division into committees, while recognizing that a presbytery may divide into commissions; the practice should remain exceptional.

**Key words:** BCO 21-4

**In the minutes:** [ga16_1988 pp. 164–165](../markdown/ga16_1988.md#ga16-p166)

---

## Verbatim record

6. That the Constitutional Inquiry, “Does BCO 21-4 forbid or permit presbyteries to divide into committees or parts in order to hear the sermons of candidates for ordination?”, referred to the Committee on Judicial Business, be answered as follows: BCO 21-4 does not permit presbyteries simply to divide into committees or parts in order to hear the sermons of candidates for ordination. It would not be contrary to the express provisions of the BCO to divide a presbytery into commissions. The General Assembly recommends that this practice not be resorted to regularly but that it be viewed as exceptional. The procedure might be subject to abuse and could be detrimental to the health and strength of the church. Also, there are more suitable alternatives. Adopted.
