## Verbatim record

Additionally, under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, several epistles end on notes of praise rather than explicit blessing, such as Jude 24- 25, 1 Peter 5:11, and 2 Peter 3:18. As seen above, Heb. 13:20-21 features both blessing and praise. If the Holy Spirit was pleased to have multiple epistles end on notes of praise to our God in addition to the multitude of benedictory endings, it seems perfectly Biblical to close public worship in this way too.

REs Martinez and Cook have argued that VPC Session believes that "Since God gets the first word when he formally summons his covenant people into his presence by his call to worship, God should also give, through the office of the minister, the last word in the form of a benediction as a formal blessing of God upon his people."[^fn-ga52-p904-n30] However, we note 2 Timothy 3:16 says that “All Scripture is breathed out by God." If a passage of Scripture is being read in worship, surely God is speaking to His people.
