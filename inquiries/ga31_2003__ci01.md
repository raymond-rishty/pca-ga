# 31-56 III.1 — Status of a case ruled judicially out of order by the SJC

*When the SJC rules a case judicially out of order, its status is substantively what it was when the presbytery completed its action.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Constitutional inquiry  ·  **Assembly:** 31st (2003)  ·  **Disposition:** advice given

*Source: [ga31_2003 p. 159](../markdown/ga31_2003.md#ga31-p161)*

---

## Digest headnote

Evangel Presbytery asked what status a case has at presbytery level after the SJC rules it “judicially out of order.” The CCB answered that the case is substantively in the status it had when the presbytery completed its action.

**Inquiry from:** Evangel Presbytery

**In the minutes:** [ga31_2003 p.159](../markdown/ga31_2003.md#ga31-p161)

---

## Verbatim record

### As referred / posed

Reference 1 from Evangel Presbytery: What is the status of a case at presbytery level that the SJC has ruled “judicially out of order?”

### CCB advice

It is the opinion of the CCB that the status of a case that the SJC has ruled “judicially out of order” is substantively that of the case at the time the presbytery completed its action. Adopted by CCB.
