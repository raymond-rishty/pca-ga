# 30-29 III — Recording All Exceptions to Presbytery and the General Assembly

*An overture requiring presbyteries to record and report all ordination exceptions to the GA was found in conflict with the Constitution because it would allow a presbytery to bind a man's conscience beyond what the BCO permits.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Overture/amendment advice  ·  **Assembly:** 30th (2002)  ·  **Provisions:** BCO 21-4, BCO Preface II.1, BCO Preface II.7  ·  **Disposition:** in conflict

*Source: [ga30_2002 lines 4687–4703](../markdown/ga30_2002.md#ga30-p103)*

## Digest headnote
*Editorial summary from the PCA Digest, Part II (Interpretations of the Constitution) — this is the Digest's wording, not the verbatim minutes. The authoritative text is the verbatim record below / linked above.*

In the opinion of the CCB, Overture 29 from Westminster Presbytery ("Amend BCO 21-4 to Record All Exceptions with Presbytery and Have Stated Clerk Report All Exceptions to the General Assembly") was in conflict with the Constitution for three reasons, including that it allowed a presbytery to determine whether a man may or may not teach what he believes, even if it is not out of accord with any fundamental of the system of doctrine, thus going beyond our constitution to bind the man's conscience (BCO Preface II.1 and II.7).

**Key words:** BCO 21-4, BCO Preface II.1, BCO Preface II.7

**Inquiry from:** Westminster Presbytery

**In the minutes:** [ga30_2002 p.103](../markdown/ga30_2002.md#ga30-p103)

As a simple way to understand, define, and explain our ministry in the PCA, we use the phrase "training and resources." The following is a summary to the Assembly.

Training

In the area of training, CE&P focuses on a variety of topics and areas. Primarily, we train leaders, both formal (ruling elders and deacons) and informal (men and women who serve the local church in a variety of roles).

CE&P continues to assist local leaders in church ministry evaluation, strategic faith planning, conflict management, and understanding our world through seminars on postmodern culture and generational demographics and characteristics. We also offer training in stewardship and worship.

The content of our teacher training workshops is organized into the categories who we teach, what we teach, why we teach, and how we teach. They also cover curriculum, individual and group learning styles, teaching methods, and small group activities. These are designed for local Sunday school and small group Bible study teachers, as well as those involved in one-on-one ministry and other classroom settings.

We have increased our training in diaconal (mercy) ministries, and have made a more intentional attempt to sensitize the church to the urgent need in this area. We have held several conferences at the assembly and local level. The most recent was the March 2001 conference in Atlanta, which we cosponsored with MNA. Over 650 people attended. We are currently planning the next conference, which will be held in Atlanta, March 13-15, 2003.

The Women in the Church arm of CE&P has provided a tremendous boost in dealing with mercy ministries and in training local women in the foundations of biblical womanhood. Through the WIC Leadership Training Seminar, 150- 200 Presbytery WIC presidents (PresWICs) and directors of women's ministry gather for a weekend of training and fellowship. Our ten WIC trainers work with local churches to help them implement a women's ministry.

## APPENDIX D

CE&P also has twenty regional trainers who interact with those who teach children, youth, and adults. They are trained by the CE&P staff to do teacher training, program evaluation and general Christian education consultation. We trained approximately three thousand people through more than one hundred training events in the past year.

At our annual Children's Ministry Conference, we bring together around 150 people involved in local church children's ministry. Through this conference, we have developed resources, networks, and encouraging friendships among those involved in this challenging ministry. We have also added a Children's Ministry Coordinator to our staff to help facilitate local church ministry to children and families.

Our youth ministry department conducts seminars to train youth workers and serves as the clearing house for children and youth ministry personnel. This year we helped over fifty churches acquire staff for their youth ministries. Churches and youth workers can use our web site to submit resumes and request information on available positions or personnel. We have also begun to establish networks among those involved in this vital ministry. Over a dozen people are involved in CE&P's internship training for youth ministries and plans are underway to expand that number.

Nearly two hundred youth from local churches participated in PYA, a weeklong leadership training conference. The conference is held at Horn Creek in Westcliffe, Colorado and Covenant College in Lookout Mountain, Georgia.

One of our concerns in ministering to families, especially children and youth, is the statistics relating to church dropouts among young adults—some who are still in high school or just entering college. We are concerned about the growing number of youth who do not see church as important, relevant, and real-life oriented. We see an increasing need to develop a biblical, reformed world and life view among all ages, but especially the younger generations.

We have also held conferences to encourage ministry couples and have conducted a conference on worship and music. CE&P plans to keep these things on our list of strategically important events.

The other arm of CE&P's ministry focuses on resources. We have six primary resources that are available to local churches.

CE&P Bookstore

Through the CE&P Bookstore, located in our office, CE&P serves churches and individuals with some of the best and the newest books available. The bookstore staff is trained to consult with local churches and customers regarding the best of materials for personal and corporate use. Through our toll-free number (1-800-283-1357) and web site (www.cepbookstore.com), the bookstore has been able to give prompt, effective service. This part of CE&P's ministry has continued to expand with a 12 percent growth during 2001-2002. The bookstore is also a key to linking local churches with CE&P staff and vice versa.

Great Commission Publications

Great Commission Publications, a major part of the publishing arm of CE&P, continues to make the best biblically reformed curriculum available to our churches. This joint venture with the Orthodox Presbyterian Church has enabled the PCA to have a full curriculum that is biblically focused, pedagogically sound, and doctrinally shaped. The GCP staff is in the process of transitioning the three-year cycle into two-year segments in response to trends in curriculum development and the needs of local churches.

Through the GCP joint venture, CE&P now offers two vacation Bible school curricula. GCP serves over two-thirds of PCA churches through the Sunday school curriculum, Trinity Hymnal, and church bulletins. The CE&P staff and regional trainers work with the GCP staff to service those products in our churches. The materials may be ordered from the CE&P Bookstore or GCP (www.cepbookstore.com, www.gcp.org).

Video Library

Several years ago, with help of the Women In the Church Love Gift and other individuals, CE&P developed a video lending library. Today over seven hundred different titles are available to more than three hundred member churches. The membership is offered at a special rate to mission churches. The Video Library catalog is now available on our website (www.pcanet.org/cep) in a searchable format. We regularly evaluate and add new titles to the inventory and are presently considering the inclusion of DVDs. The staff is available to advise and consult with local churches over the resources available through the Video Library.
