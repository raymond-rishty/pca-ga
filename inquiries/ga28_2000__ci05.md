# 28-19 V — Re-ordination of Former PCA Ministers

*The CCB found no technical constitutional conflict but raised concerns about the nature of ordination and inconsistency between restoration after deposition versus divestiture.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Overture/amendment advice  ·  **Assembly:** 28th (2000)  ·  **Provisions:** BCO 34-10, BCO 21-5, BCO 36-7, BCO 37-5  ·  **Disposition:** not in technical conflict

*Source: [ga28_2000 lines 3866–3870](../markdown/ga28_2000.md#ga28-p77)*

## Digest headnote
*Editorial summary from the PCA Digest, Part II (Interpretations of the Constitution) — this is the Digest's wording, not the verbatim minutes. The authoritative text is the verbatim record below / linked above.*

In the opinion of the CCB, Overture 13 from Susquehanna Valley Presbytery ("Re-ordination of Former PCA Ministers") was not in technical conflict with any other provisions of the Constitution. However, the Committee pointed out that BCO 34-10 does not mandate divestiture in every case and was also concerned that the language proposed for the new BCO 21-5 raised questions about the nature and importance of the call to the ministry ("...simply a lack of call...."). Finally, the Committee noted the apparent inconsistency in the handling of one who had been removed from office by discipline (deposition, BCO 36-7) who would be restored as per BCO 37-5, and one who is divested without censure (BCO 34-10) who could be restored only after re-examination and re-ordination.

**Key words:** BCO 34-10, BCO 21-5, BCO 36-7, BCO 37-5

**Inquiry from:** Susquehanna Valley Presbytery

The GA/MNA Permanent Committee recommends approval of the following response:

This instruction to the GA/MNA Committee was issued in response to a specific instance of a woman teaching in an GA/MNA conference in February 1999. For this reason, and because GA/MNA does not possess the authority to select or assign people to other types of speaking and teaching ministries (except assigning teaching elders as church planters), we understand the parameters of our response to be limited to the issue of choosing women for speaking and teaching in seminars, conferences and worship services conducted under the auspices of GA/MNA.

GA/MNA conducts two kinds of activities at its conferences and seminars that are addressed by this motion. One activity is worship. The Scriptures, as interpreted by the subordinate standards, give sufficient standards for the ordering of public worship. The preaching of the Word in worship is to be protected, and that protection is assured as the preaching of the Word is conducted by elders. GA/MNA adheres to this standard in the worship services that it conducts.

The other activity is seminars designed to provide church planters and other leaders with greater insights and skills for their ministry. While GA/MNA is careful to assure that worthy material is presented in these seminars, the context is not that of a worship service, and therefore the subject matter does not require the same degree of protection as the content of worship.

The primary difference between the preaching of the Word in worship and the subject matter of seminars has at its heart the issue of authority. The content of seminars is not intended to carry the weight to which the description "teach or have authority over" (I Timothy 2:11-12) would apply. The context of this passage is the Apostle's presentation of instructions for the proper conduct of public worship and not that of the more informal seminar which is generally more subjective, informal and based on personal experience than is the preaching of the Word in worship.

In most cases, teaching and ruling elders present the content in the seminar context. However, from time to time, GA/MNA may ask that godly men and women give presentations even though they are not elders, but who in God's providence may have insights and experience which may prove helpful and instructive to those who lead in ministry.
