# 29-28 III.2 — Requirement that Candidates or Officers Receive More than a Majority

*A Session cannot raise the voting requirement beyond a majority for calling a pastor, dissolving a pastoral relation, or electing a church officer.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Constitutional inquiry  ·  **Assembly:** 29th (2001)  ·  **Provisions:** BCO 20-4, BCO 24-4  ·  **Disposition:** advice given

*Source: [ga29_2001 lines 5866–5871](../markdown/ga29_2001.md#ga29-p134)*

---

## Digest headnote
*Editorial summary from the PCA Digest, Part II (Interpretations of the Constitution) — this is the Digest's wording, not the verbatim minutes. The authoritative text is the verbatim record below / linked above.*

The CCB answered a non-judicial reference from Eastern Carolina Presbytery, by stating that a Session cannot change the voting requirements of BCO 20-4 and BCO 24-4 by increasing the number of voters beyond a majority needed to call a pastor, dissolve a pastoral relationship, or elect a church officer.

**Key words:** BCO 20-4, BCO 24-4

**Inquiry from:** Eastern Carolina Presbytery

**In the minutes:** [ga29_2001 p.134](../markdown/ga29_2001.md#ga29-p134)

COMMUNICATION 5 From Westminster Presbytery (to MNA) "Erect New Presbytery"

Dear Dr. Taylor, May 16, 2001 At a Called Meeting of Westminster Presbytery on May 15, 2001, the Presbytery took action to withdraw from the Presbyterian Church in America, effective at its January Stated Meeting, and to overture the PCA General Assembly to recognize this fact and constitute a new Presbytery, with the same geographic boundaries, out of the Teaching Elders and Churches that do not desire to leave the PCA. A copy of the Presbytery's action is enclosed with this letter.
