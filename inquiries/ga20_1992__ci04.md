# 20-75 — Right to complain against a court's reception of an intemperate protest

*A presbyter may complain against a court's decision to receive a protest alleged to contain intemperate or disrespectful language, since that reception is itself a court action under BCO 43-1.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Constitutional inquiry  ·  **Assembly:** 20th (1992)  ·  **Provisions:** BCO 43-1, BCO 45-5  ·  **Disposition:** advice given

*Source: [ga20_1992 lines 14297–14339](../markdown/ga20_1992.md#ga20-p267)*

---

## Digest headnote
*Editorial summary from the PCA Digest, Part II (Interpretations of the Constitution) — this is the Digest's wording, not the verbatim minutes. The authoritative text is the verbatim record below / linked above.*

1992, p. 267, 20-75. 'Does a member of presbytery have a constitutional right to complain that presbytery has received a protest containing allegedly intemperate or slanderous language?' RESPONSE: Yes. BCO 43-1 states that a complaint is a written representation made against some act or decision of a court of the church. The decision to receive a protest is a decision or action of a court and, therefore, may be complained against. However, BCO 45-5 states that a protest must be couched in temperate language and be respectful to the court. Therefore, although the recording of the protest itself is to end the matter, if a presbyter is convinced that the protest was not couched in temperate language or respectful to the court, he may complain against the action of the court in receiving it. Here the matter that is complained against is new (i.e. intemperate language or disrespect) and is not a continuation of the matter about which the protest was submitted.

**Key words:** BCO 43-1, BCO 45-5
