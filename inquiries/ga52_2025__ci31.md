# IV.A — Questions regarding Highlands Presbytery's compliance with SJC Case No. 2023-16 (scope of SJC remit and authority)

*The CCB declined a reference from Highlands Presbytery, ruling it lacks authority to render opinions on prior SJC decisions, which are final decisions of the General Assembly.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Constitutional inquiry  ·  **Assembly:** 52nd (2025)  ·  **Provisions:** BCO 41-3, BCO 39-3.1, BCO 43-10, BCO 40-2, BCO 15-5  ·  **Disposition:** CCB declined to accede to the reference; SJC decisions are final decisions of the GA (BCO 15-5); CCB does not render opinions on previous SJC decisions; Adopted 8-0-0

The prayer ended, let a psalm be sung, if with convenience it may be done. After which (unless some other ordinance of Christ, that concerns the congregation at that time, be to follow) let the minister dismiss the congregation with a solemn blessing [emphasis added].

In the 1645 Directory section titled, "Sanctification of the Lord's Day," we read:

That all the people meet so timely for public worship, that the whole congregation may be present at the beginning, and with one heart solemnly join together in all parts of the public worship, and not depart until after the blessing [emphasis added].

The 1879 PCUS BCO contained the following (in what is now BCO 8-5):

> When a minister is called to labor as a Pastor, it belongs to his office to pray for and with his flock, as the mouth of the people unto God; to feed the flock by reading, expounding, and preaching the Word; to direct the congregation in singing the praises of God; to administer the sacraments; to bless the people from God;... [emphasis added].

In his 1898 Exposition on this section of the 1879 PCUS Book, F.P. Ramsay wrote:

It belongs to the Pastor to bless the people from God; wherefore the benediction is by him pronounced as an official declaration of the divine mind [emphasis added].

In light of these considerations, we cannot say that the Session's decision in this case that a benediction rather than a doxology is to be used at the end of the worship service was a clear error of liturgical judgment under the standard of BCO 39-3.3, despite the fact that its longtime Senior Pastor disagreed. The Session has the power to establish rules for worship, and with that power came the discretion to set the policy about benedictions, a practice supported by the historic authorities cited above. Decisions about the rules for worship remain within the discretionary authority of a Session, even if Presbytery or other
