# 20-62 — Minister from another denomination serving as stated supply

*A minister from another denomination licensed by a PCA presbytery may serve as stated supply but may not administer sacraments unless his denomination has fraternal relations with the PCA or his sacramental views are consistent with PCA standards.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Constitutional inquiry  ·  **Assembly:** 20th (1992)  ·  **Provisions:** BCO 19-1, BCO 22-5, BCO 22-6  ·  **Disposition:** advice given

*Source: [ga20_1992 minutes p.127 / PDF p.129](../markdown/ga20_1992.md#ga20-p129)*

## Digest headnote
*Editorial summary from the PCA Digest, Part II (Interpretations of the Constitution) — this is the Digest's wording, not the verbatim minutes. The authoritative text is the verbatim record below / linked above.*

"May a minister from another denomination, duly licensed by a presbytery (BCO 19-1), serve as a stated supply (BCO 22-5, 22-6)? May such a minister administer the sacraments? This inquiry comes from a real situation within the James River Presbytery which needs a timely answer to this perplexed question. The advice will go to the Ministerial and Church Relations Committee and, through it, to the Presbytery." RESPONSE: The Committee on Constitutional Business answers Personal Resolution #3, constitutional inquiry, with the following: 1. Yes. A minister from another denomination, recognized by presbytery as such and duly licensed by presbytery, may serve as a stated supply (BCO 19-1, 22-5). 2. No. Unless he is a minister in a church with which the PCA had fraternal relations or the presbytery has determined that his view of the sacraments are consistent with the Standards of the PCA.

**Key words:** BCO 19-1, BCO 22-5, BCO 22-6

**Inquiry from:** James River Presbytery

2. No. Unless he is a minister in a church with which the PCA had fraternal relations or the presbytery has determined that his view of the sacraments are consistent with the Standards of the PCA.
