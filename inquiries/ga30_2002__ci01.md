# 30-29 III — Overture to abolish the Standing Judicial Commission

*The CCB found the overture to abolish the SJC in conflict with BCO 14-1 and 15, and also unconstitutionally vague about the basis for alternative decisions.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Overture/amendment advice  ·  **Assembly:** 30th (2002)  ·  **Provisions:** BCO 14-1, BCO 15, BCO 15-4, BCO 15-5  ·  **Disposition:** in conflict

*Source: [ga30_2002 lines 2203–2203](../markdown/ga30_2002.md#ga30-p102)*

## Digest headnote
*Editorial summary from the PCA Digest, Part II (Interpretations of the Constitution) — this is the Digest's wording, not the verbatim minutes. The authoritative text is the verbatim record below / linked above.*

In the opinion of the CCB, Overture 6 from Westminster Presbytery ("Revise BCO 15-4 and BCO 15-5 to Abolish the Standing Judicial Commission") was in conflict with other parts of the constitution, including but not limited to BCO 14-1, 15. The overture was also unconstitutionally vague as to what will be the basis of making the alternative decisions indicated in the proposed BCO 15-5.

**Key words:** BCO 14-1, BCO 15, BCO 15-4, BCO 15-5

**Inquiry from:** Westminster Presbytery

IV. Items Discussed and Actions Taken by the Committee

- **A.** A Rationale to North American Presbyterian and Reformed Council (NAPARC) for the termination of the membership of the Christian Reformed Church in North America in NAPARC be prepared and submitted. The CRCNA delegation to NAPARC in 2000 requested that the PCA delegation present to NAPARC in 2001 a Rationale for the Termination of the CRCNA's membership in NAPARC. The IRC prepared such a rationale (see "Attachment A" below) and presented it to the 2001 meeting of NAPARC. Your IRC believes that the CRCNA circumvented its constitution to effect the ordination of women as ministers and ruling elders.

In 1995 the General Synod of the CRCNA approved a motion that the word "male" in the section of the CRCNA Church Order, Article 3-a, that restricted the offices of minister and ruling elder to men be "declared inoperative" by classes (presbyteries) who wished to do so. The action was taken by a vote of a General Synod rather than through the process of actually revising the

Church Order by the ordinary process. As a result, NAPARC began the process in 1996 to suspend the CRCNA from voting privileges in NAPARC. In 1998, the suspension went into effect. CRCNA Synod 2000 reaffirmed the controversial decision of Synod 1995.

At the 2000 meeting of NAPARC, after consideration of the termination of the membership of the CRCNA in NAPARC (initiated by the PCA), the matter was deferred to the 2001 meeting with the request that the Presbyterian Church in America delegation provide a specific rationale for the proposed action. A Rationale was prepared by the PCA-IRC and mailed to the NAPARC delegations before the 2001 council meeting. The CRCNA presented their response at the 2001 meeting (see "Attachment B").

At the 2000 meeting, the CRCNA delegation offered several reasons why the CRCNA should not be dismissed from NAPARC. Their principal arguments were 1) A Theological-historical Argument, based on the Belgic Confession Article XXX, 2) A Constitutional Argument based on NAPARC's article II. Basis of the Council, (the inerrancy and authority of Scripture and doctrinal integrity expressed through Reformed doctrinal standards) and 3) Pastoral concerns regarding the past, present and future relationship of CRCNA churches, ministers, officers and members with one another should the CRCNA be dismissed from

### NAPARC.

The CRCNA delegation argued that the French and Latin versions of the Belgic confession, Article XXX used generic, rather than gender-specific terms. In other words, that "persons" [both male and female] could be ministers and ruling elders in the Church, not just "men." The CRCNA delegation did not argue from the whole of Article XXX that focuses on who is fit for ministry. The PCA delegation, in response, argued, that the Christian Reformed Church, by reaffirming its stand in favor of the ordination of women, has acted contrarily to Article XXX of the Belgic Confession, which states concerning the government of, and offices in, the church: "By these means every thing will be carried on in the Church with good order and decency, when faithful men are chosen, according to the rule prescribed by St. Paul to Timothy." submit the matter to the General Assemblies and General Synods for action on the following grounds:

We believe that the CRCNA has had opportunity over the last several years to reverse the controversial action of CRCNA Synod 1995 in response to NAPARC's suspension of the CRCNA from votingmember status in NAPARC. In 1996 the process was begun in NAPARC (initiated by the PCA) to suspend the CRCNA from NAPARC membership after CRCNA Synod 1995 had approved the ordination of women to the offices of ruling elder and minister. The underlying hermeneutical issue was cited by the PCA in correspondence between the two highest judicatories of the PCA and CRCNA (Minutes of the 25th General Assembly of the PCA, 1997, pp. 375- 383). In 1998, the suspension from membership was effected. CRCNA Synod 2000 did not reverse but reaffirmed and approved the ordination of women to the offices of ruling elder and minister.

We believe that the CRCNA has acted contrarily to the NAPARC constitution (II. Basis of the Council), against the teaching of the Word of God and the Belgic Confession, Article XXX by declaring the word male in Article 3-a of the Church Order to be inoperative and authorizing the churches under its jurisdiction (should they desire) to ordain and install women in the offices of elder, minister, and evangelist, thus circumventing her own constitution.

The General Assemblies and General Synods of the NAPARCmember denominations must now vote on the issue within the next three years for it to go into effect.
