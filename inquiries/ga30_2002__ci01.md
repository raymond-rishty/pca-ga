# 30-29 III — Procedure for Dealing with Alleged Conflicts Between Scripture and the Constitution of the PCA

*The CCB found Eastern Canada Presbytery's overture in conflict with the Constitution because it would allow a General Assembly to bypass constitutional processes and permit modification of commission reports.*

**Body:** Committee on Constitutional Business (CCB)  ·  **Type:** Overture/amendment advice  ·  **Assembly:** 30th (2002)  ·  **Provisions:** BCO 26-1, BCO 26-2, BCO 15-1, BCO 15-5  ·  **Disposition:** in conflict

*Source: [ga30_2002 minutes p.102 / PDF p.104](../markdown/ga30_2002.md#ga30-p104)*

## Digest headnote
*Editorial summary from the PCA Digest, Part II (Interpretations of the Constitution) — this is the Digest's wording, not the verbatim minutes. The authoritative text is the verbatim record below / linked above.*

In the opinion of the CCB, Overture 27 from Eastern Canada Presbytery ("Add to BCO 26-1 Procedure for Dealing with Alleged Conflicts Between Scripture and the Constitution of the PCA") was in conflict with other parts of the Constitution. Specific areas of conflict included, but were not limited to the following: 1) the Overture would have allowed a General Assembly to avoid the provisions of the BCO without appropriate constitutional process and would have resulted in serious constitutional uncertainty (BCO 26-2), and 2) the Overture was in conflict with BCO 15.1 and BCO 15.5, in that it allowed for modification of a commission report.

**Key words:** BCO 26-1, BCO 26-2, BCO 15-1, BCO 15-5

**Inquiry from:** Eastern Canada Presbytery

## Verbatim record

Overture 27 from Eastern Canada Presbytery: Add to BCO 26-1 Procedure for Dealing with Alleged Conflicts Between Scripture and the Constitution of the PCA It is the opinion of the CCB that the Overture is in conflict with other parts of the Constitution. Specific areas of conflict include, but are not limited to:

1. The Overture would allow a General Assembly to avoid the provisions of the BCO without appropriate constitutional process and would result in serious constitutional uncertainty. (BCO 26-2).

2. The Overture allows modification of virtually any action of the General Assembly or commission thereof, with the sole limitation that an actual judgment of a judicial commission may not be changed or deleted, thereby undermining the very principle of orderly Church Government which the BCO is designed to protect. [See BCO Preface II.2].

3. The Overture is in conflict with BCO 15.1 and 15.5, in that it allows for modification of a commission report.

4. The Overture requires the CCB to propose business, inconsistent with RAO 7-2 and the Operations Manual for CCB.

5. Finally, reference is made to the recommendation of the CCB with respect to Constitutional Inquiry #4 of the 29th General Assembly: "The presumption that we need to suspend our rules in order to obey Christ is an opinion that the CCB is not willing to affirm. If our standards
